# HexCreeper — design notes

Every rule in the game, its default numbers, and why it is the way it is. The numbers marked *(setting)* are defaults that a map can set: its rules, or its components' attrs (see Settings).

## Core rules
- World: a **hex grid**, effectively infinite (integer coordinates clamped to ±1,000,000). The core starts at (0,0), the centre of the first quadrant.
- Turn-based. On your turn your pieces act in any order, each as often as its **action points** allow (see Action points). Placing pieces and paid heals are also allowed during the turn and cost no points. The turn ends with End turn (Enter / `.`), or automatically once no piece can act and you can't afford a placement. Then the phases: every enemy squad acts (enemies); the turn counter advances, tiles whose countdown has run out are gone, and enemies may spawn (environment); and every piece gets back action points (housekeeping).
- Pieces have a type (which sets how they move), an owner (player or enemy), HP, a level, stats and xp (see Stats and combat), a rank and an optional link parent. Both sides use link trees.
- Moving onto an opposing piece is an attack: 1 damage.
- **Advance after a kill** (always, since 2026-11-25, when the `advance` setting went: Attack is how to strike without moving): if a player attack kills, or breaks a solid or fixture, and the whole moving group can then legally step in that direction (every destination cell empty or vacated by the group, link range respected, no harm tile you know of), the group steps forward as part of the same action, setting off any tiles it lands on. Otherwise the attackers stay put. Enemies never advance after kills.
- The game is over when your original core is destroyed.

## Grid
- A hex grid: pointy-top hexes in axial coordinates (q, r), 6 neighbours, hex distance (used for link range, spawning and pathing). Link range 3 is a hexagon of 37 cells.
- Quadrants are hexagonal chunks of radius 5 = 91 cells *(setting)*, each with 6 neighbours. Chunks of radius k tile the plane on the lattice (2k+1, −k), (k, k+1), and chunk (0,0) is centred on the start cell.
- Keys: Q E / A D / Z C (NW NE / W E / SW SE), numpad 7 9 4 6 1 3. W / X (numpad 8 / 2) go up / down, zigzagging: left (Q or Z) and right (E or C) in turn, or the other side if the next is blocked; every step makes the other side next, so W then X comes back (2026-11-22). Space (or . or numpad 5) ends the turn, S recenters, Enter opens the selected piece's menu (arrows and Tab move between its items; Enter or Space picks one), Tab cycles your pieces, Esc cancels. The menu items' keys: P place, V swap, J jump, L attach, B detach, H heal, R recover, U quicken, G go to, O explore. (Before 2026-11-22: Enter ended the turn, Space recentered, ← / → moved, S swapped and X detached.)
- History: there was also a square grid (8 neighbours, Chebyshev distance, 5×5 quadrants). After playtesting both, hex "works quite well" and felt better, so as of 2026-11-18 the game is hex only. The chunk radius default went from 2 to 3 along the way, and to 5 with border walls (2026-11-19), for bigger rooms.

## Gold
- **Gold** (2026-11-25; it replaced score) is what you have to spend. **Only loot gives gold**: kills give XP and nothing else (more for a multi-kill), and healing nobody needs stays in the heal. `Game.lootTaken` is all the gold you've looted, spent or not (the end-of-game message gives it). It pays for Place.
- Since 2026-11-27 gold is an **item** (`gold_coin`, `ITEM_GOLD`: see Items and inventories), so it takes room and has weight: loot gives its coins to the piece that takes it (what it can't carry falls on the ground there), your gold is what your pieces carry (`Items.goldHeld`, shown in the header), and Place spends it from the parent's first, then your other pieces' (`Items.spendGold`). Before, it was one number, `Game.gold`.

## Items and inventories
- 2026-11-27 (`js/hc/model/Items.js`, `data/items.json5`), from Ouroboros's items and inventories. An **item kind** has a name, plural, material, volume (cm³), weight (g: volume × the material's density if not given), capacity (room it takes, 1), stack (most in a stack, 1) and maybe an inventory (its limits). An **item** is `{id, kind, count, inventory?, name?}` (its own ids, `Game.nextItemId`).
- An **inventory** is `{items:[...]}` on a **holder**: a piece, a cell's ground (`Game.ground`, kept only while it holds something) or an item, so they **nest** (a corpse holding a pouch holding coins). Limits: capacity (a stack takes its kind's capacity once), weight (with what's inside) and volume (an item's own: a container's volume should allow for what it holds). Pieces' come from rules of stats (`carryCapacity` **10 + str / 2**, `carryWeight` **2500 × str** g, `carryVolume` **2000 × con** cm³); the ground's are rules (`groundCapacity` 1000, 10 t, 10 m³); an item's, its kind's (none given: unlimited). Nothing goes in itself. Stacks merge up to their size; a named item (a corpse) doesn't stack.
- **Corpses**: a piece that dies leaves its species' `corpse` (an item kind, `corpse` if not given; `none`: just what it had) on its cell, holding everything it had, regardless of limits, named for it ("goblin corpse", "corpse of Tess"). Small species leave `small_corpse`, ogres `large_corpse`, creepers a `husk`.
- An enemy's profile can give it `items` (ids, or `{kind, count}` with count n or [min, max], rolled per piece from the seed).
- **Reach** (2026-11-27, `Items.reachHolders`): what a piece of yours can get at is what it carries (ref `piece`), the ground under it (`ground`), and the **containers** you know of on its cell or next to it (`fixture:x,y`; what's in one only while its kind's `access` allows), and everything in those, all the way down. It can **take** any of that (into what it carries), **drop** it (onto the ground under it), or **transfer** it (into any other holder it can reach: an item with an inventory, by id), as much of a stack as fits, for `takeCost`, `dropCost` and `transferCost` action points (**0.25**, **0.15**, **0.25**: rules of level and stats, at least `MIN_COST`; take and drop were an action at first). **Take all** takes what's directly in a holder, a thing at a time (each a take), while its points last, leaving what doesn't fit. None of them keeps the turn from ending by itself.
- **The Inventory dialog** (H): a section for each holder in reach (Carrying, On the ground, then each container: "Chest (here)", "Chest (east)"; a shut one says so and hides what's in it), each with its totals against its limits (the ground's just its totals), and Take all. What's in a thing is under it, indented, behind a ▸/▾; a container fixture's section has one too. Each container keeps whether it's expanded (`expanded`, saved, via `Items.toggleExpanded`, which costs nothing): an item is collapsed unless it's true (a corpse is left expanded), a fixture expanded unless it's false. Rows have Take (or Drop, for what you carry), Take all (for a container) and Transfer, which turns the dialog into picking where: each holder and container gets a Here (disabled with why, if it can't go there), Cancel or Esc leaves it. It stays open when the turn ends (it closes only when the game is over or the piece is gone).
- Weights and volumes show in kg and litres, or pounds and US gallons (the Units preference). Saves (version 8) keep pieces' inventories, the ground and `nextItemId`.
- **Container fixtures** (2026-11-27): a fixture kind with an `inventory` (limits, as an item kind's), `contents` (what each starts with, as an enemy profile's items, rolled from the generator's rng when it's made) and `access` (a formula of its state: when what's in it can be got at; default 1). A container that's gone (broken) leaves what was in it on the ground. **Chest** (oak, 20 HP, in the way, open/close, access `open`: up to 40 gold, maybe a dagger, a short sword, a belt pouch), **Cabinet** (oak, 15 HP, in the way, open/close: jerky, maybe a club) and **Table** (pine, 10 HP, ×2 to cross, always open: jerky, a little gold). The caverns and endless maps' `featureTable` is barricade 3, chest 1, cabinet 1, table 1. Saves keep fixtures' inventories.
- **Equipment** (2026-11-27): slots `EQUIP_SLOTS` (weapon, offhand, body, head, hands, feet). An item kind's `equip` is `{slot, damage, penetration, armor}`. A piece equips what it carries itself (not inside something), at most one per slot (`equipped:true` on the item, saved; equipped items don't stack), for its kind's `equip.cost` action points, or `equipCost` (**0.5**) if it has none (`Combat.equipCostOf`: weapons, a helm and gloves 0.25, a shield and boots 0.5, body armour 1), swapping out what was in the slot; Unequip costs the same. Its **weapon**'s damage and penetration **replace** its own (species or profile damage, profile penetration: what it does unarmed; `damageBonus` and `penetrationBonus` still apply), and everything equipped **adds** its armor (`Combat.weaponOf`, `armorOf`, used by `damageRange`, `penetrationOf` and the attacks). Moving an item anywhere (take, drop, transfer) or dying unequips it. **Enemies equip** what their profile gives them (`Items.equipAll`); the reavers' weapons match their old profile damage (goblin reaver: short sword 2–6; hobgoblin reaver: long sword 2–8; hobgoblin: club 1–6), so the sim is unchanged. Weapons: dagger 1–4 pen 1, short sword 2–6 pen 1, long sword 2–8 pen 2, club 1–6. Armour: wooden shield (offhand) 1, leather armour (body) 1, chain shirt (body) 2, iron helm (head) 1, leather gloves and boots 0 (for now). Chests and cabinets may hold armour. The Inventory dialog has Equip / Unequip on what a piece carries, and shows "chain shirt (body)"; the character sheet lists what's equipped, and its damage, penetration and armor count it.
- **Starting items** (2026-11-27): a background's `items` (as an enemy profile's). Your character starts with them (`Combat.giveBackgroundItems`, from `makeCharacter`, and for a core with no character, its default background's), equipped where they can be; an enemy gets its background's as well as its profile's (its profile's first, so they get the slots). Placed pieces start with nothing. Species' unarmed damage is low (human 1–2), so the playable backgrounds give a weapon (most) and some gold or gear: urchin a dagger and jerky, soldier a short sword, leather armour and 5 gold, noble a long sword and 20 gold, and so on. The creation sheet shows what the character will have equipped.
- The Inventory dialog's footer has **Character** and the character sheet's **Inventory** (not while creating), each switching to the other for the same piece.
- To come: locks (and keys) on containers; offhand weapons and two-handed ones.

## Needs: thirst, hunger, fatigue
- 2026-11-27 (`js/hc/model/Needs.js`, a module in Game; `NEEDS`, `NEED_UNIT`). **Only your pieces** have them (`piece.needs`, {need:value} in hundredths, saved; enemies have none). Each starts at its **upper**.
- **Bounds** are rules of the piece's own level and stats (not what needs make them): `minThirst` 0, `lowerThirst` 25 × con/10, `upperThirst` 75 × con/10, `maxThirst` 100 × con/10 (likewise Hunger, Fatigue). Values are kept within them. When a piece's stats change (levelling: `Combat.changeStats`), each need keeps its place between its min and max (`Needs.rescaleNeeds`, 2026-11-28: half full stays half full); the levelling sheet's preview shows it.
- **Ticks** (added; below 0): `thirstTurnTick` −0.25, `hungerTurnTick` −0.25, `fatigueTurnTick` −0.1 at the end of each turn (housekeeping, before AP recovery); `thirstApTick` −0.05, `hungerApTick` −0.05, `fatigueApTick` −0.15 per action point spent (`Combat.spend`). About 100–200 turns from upper to lower.
- **Levels**: min (at min), low (≤ lower), high (> upper), else mid. Low/high each need adds its `…LowRecovery`/`…HighRecovery` to AP recovery, `…LowHp`/`…HighHp` to HP each turn (fractions carry in `piece.hpCarry`; HP lost this way is damage and can kill, unattributed), and `…LowStats`/`…HighStats` to every stat (`Needs.statVars`, used by all stat-based rules but the needs' own). At min, low effects × `…MinMultiplier` (2). Defaults: thirst −0.25/+0.1 recovery; hunger −0.25/+0.1 HP; fatigue nothing yet (rest to come).
- **Eating and drinking**: an item kind's `quench`, `satiates`, `heals` and `rests` (2026-11-28: points per dose to thirst, hunger, HP and fatigue, may be negative: jerky is salty). Each is a number (any species) or **by species**, `{"goblin":100, "hobgoblin":90, "*":5}`: the eater's species' own value, else that of the nearest species it extends (`Subtypes.lineage`), else `"*"`, else 0 (`Items.consumeEffects`). So enemies carry food that's no use to you: goblins and hobgoblins have **mold cake** (100 for a goblin, 5 for most), and the urchin background no longer gives jerky. **Crawler stalk** (tables, cabinets) satiates 50 and heals 2.5 (HP fractions carry, `Needs.changeHp`; negative heals can kill). The Eat/Drink tooltips say what it would do for this piece. `dose` (how many at once; what's left does proportionally less), `consumeCost` (else the `consumeCost` rule, 0.25). Eat/Drink anything in reach (Inventory dialog).
- **Fluids**: an item kind with `fluid:true` (water: 1 per mL, a 250 mL dose quenches 25) goes only in an inventory with `fluids:true` (flask 250 mL, canteen 1 L, water skin 2 L; capacity 1), and nothing else goes there (`addProblem` 'fluid'/'solid'); fluids can't be taken or dropped, only transferred between flasks. Floors can have a `fluid` (stream, pool: water): from on or next to one, a piece can **Drink** (the dialog's section "Stream (east)") or **Fill** a flask it can reach (`fillCost` 0.5). Item lists (profiles, backgrounds, fixture contents) can give `contents`: every playable background starts with a full canteen; cabinets may hold a full flask.
- The character sheet shows Thirst, Hunger, Fatigue ("62/100 · Parched"); the hover and Status show the words for needs not in the middle.
- History: before, score came from kills (a combo: n kills in one move scored n × their total score value, n² for 1-point enemies, counting pieces destroyed below a killed one), from healing nobody needed (`healScore`, 1 a point) and from loot, and the header showed it as *to spend / earned in all*. There was a heal combo before 2026-11-21 too.

## Rank (both sides)
- Each piece's rank = max(base, 1 + min(rank of its two highest-ranked children)); a missing child counts as 0. Base = 1. So a piece needs **two** children to rank up, and a full binary tree maximises rank but is the most fragile.
- Since 2026-11-24 HP, costs and spawn pressure come from **level** (see Stats and combat), not rank, and how often a piece acts from its action points. Since 2026-11-26 rank sets how XP is shared along links (see XP sharing); more rank-based features are to come.
- Ranks are recomputed whenever a piece is placed, spawned or destroyed.

## Stats and combat
(2026-11-24, `model/Combat.js`; the direction: a single piece worth playing first, then parties, then rank features, then link structures again.)
- **Species** (2026-11-26, `species` in `data/entities.json5`, `pkg.species`): what a creature is, a template for it. For each stat in `stats`, a **range** and a **typical** value (a profile's, if it doesn't say): `[min, typical, max]`, or `[min, max]` for a typical value half way, `Math.round((min + max) / 2)`; `[3, 10, 18]` if not given (a species going down to 0 should say so). Entries can `extends` another (a sub-species: a hobgoblin could be a goblin with its own str), a stat's whole entry replacing the other's (so a new range without a typical value gets the half way one). A species also has a base **damage**, `[min, max]` (`[1, 4]` if not given; a profile's unless it gives its own), **playable** (`true` or `false`, false if not given): whether it can be chosen for a character (at character creation, to come), and maybe **backgrounds**, the ids of the backgrounds it can go with (any, if not given). Playable: human, dwarf (and **mountain dwarf**), elf (and **high elf**, **wood elf**; sub-species by `extends`, changing a few stats), **halfling** and **gnome** (small: str up to 16, damage 1–4), **half-elf**, **half-orc** (damage 1–6); every playable one's typical stats add up to 80. Not playable: goblin (its old profile stats as typical, ranges around them), **hobgoblin** (a bigger goblin, damage 1–6), **kobold** (damage 1–3), **orc** (up to 19), **ogre** (str and con 10–22, damage 2–8), creeper (str, agl, con `[3, 8, 18]`; may become undead later). (2026-11-26: the new ones are placeholders to tune.) A piece has its species' id (`piece.species`, saved; save version 5); its stats can't go above its max (`statRange`, `spendStatPoints`, the sheet's +), and character creation will keep them above its min. The character sheet's title gives it (Your core · Human · Level 2). More will come from species later.
- **Backgrounds** (2026-11-26, `backgrounds` in `data/entities.json5`, `pkg.backgrounds`; they replaced `characters`, the adventurer class): where a creature comes from. A name and description, **playable** (false if not given), maybe **species**, the ids of the species it can go with (any, if not given), and **statAdj** (2026-11-26), `{stat:n}`, what it adds to its species' typical stats (negative to lower one): the **baseline** a creature of the pair starts from (`baselineStats`, each kept in the species' range). Creation's limits are around the baseline, and an enemy's stats are its baseline with its profile's own over it. Later base skills and abilities. Each playable background's adjustments add up to 0: urchin (dex, wil +1; int, cha −1: on stats the rules don't use yet, so the test character, a human urchin, plays as a plain human), reaver (str +2; wis, cha −1), soldier, acolyte, scholar, noble, hunter, thief, artisan, hermit, sailor, entertainer, merchant, farmhand, pit fighter, clan smith (dwarves and gnomes only), forest warden (elves only); denizen (enemies' default) has none. A species and a background **fit** (`backgroundFits`) if each allows the other: the intersection, which data can leave empty (the designer's to fix). Urchin (playable), denizen (not: enemies' default) and reaver (playable). A piece has its background's id (`piece.background`, saved; save version 6); the character sheet's title gives it (Your core · Human · Urchin · Level 1).
- **Your pieces** have no profile of their own (everything from their species): they're the species and background `giveProfile(p, undefined, speciesId, backgroundId)` is given, if there's such a species and the background is playable and fits, else the first playable species (human) and the first playable background that fits it (urchin). Your core is the character you create; pieces you place are of its species and background (typical stats, no name).
- **Character creation** (2026-11-26; the character sheet's create mode, `CharacterSheet.show(null, {create, ...})`, App `createCharacter`): after Start in a map's brief, before the game begins (Cancel goes back to the brief). A **name** (1 to 64 letters, not all space), a **description** (up to 1024, may be empty), a **species** and a **background** (drop-down lists of the playable ones; one that fits none of the other's playable ones is disabled, and choosing one the other doesn't fit changes the other to the first that does; either change puts the stats back to the species' typical ones), and the **stats**, with + and −: **`creationPoints`** (2) to spend, each stat at most **`creationNegAdj`** (2) below its species' typical value and **`creationPosAdj`** (3) above (and in its species' range), lowering one giving a point back (map rules, the new map's: `creationRange`, `creationPoints`). The other lines preview the character (`characterPiece`), changes in green. Confirm once it's named and every point is in (`characterProblem` is the model's check); it's remembered (localStorage `hc.character`) to start the next creation from. The core gets it at `Game.reset` (`game.character`, `makeCharacter`): its `name` and `description` are saved with it (save version 7), its name is used for it in the combat log, hover and sheet title, and the sheet shows both at the top (the description editable). Tests create `TEST_CHARACTER` (Tess, a human urchin with int and cha a point up), confirmed by `waitForGame` and `pressStart`.
- **Profiles** (`data/entities.json5`): an enemy's, from `enemies` (its id; a **creeper** if it has none). A profile has **species** (an id, or a list of them: the species it can be, a piece being the first unless `giveProfile(p, id, speciesId)` is given another of them; required: a profile without one that's there is left out, with an error), **backgrounds** (the ones it can have, `["denizen"]` if not given: each piece gets one that fits its species at random, seeded by its id, or the one it's given if that's one of them; none, with an error, if none fit; the goblin reaver is a reaver), its own **stats** (str, agl, con, dex, int, wis, wil, cha; a piece gets its species' typical values for any it doesn't give; they must be within every one of its species' ranges, or it's left out), a **level** (1), a weapon's **damage** range (its species' base damage if not given), **penetration**, **armor** (damage reduction), an optional fixed **hp**, and for enemies the **xp** it's worth (else `xpValue`, `5 × level`). A piece gets a copy, saved with it, with its own xp. Stats: str melee accuracy, damage and penetration; agl defence (and later speed); con HP (and later stamina); wis later initiative; dex later ranged accuracy; int, wil and cha for later.
- **An attack** (moving onto an enemy, as before): a **d100** roll that hits if it's no more than the hit chance, `hitChance` (`50 + 3 × (accuracy − defence)`, kept from `hitMin` 5 to `hitMax` 95), with accuracy `meleeAccuracy` (`str + level`) and defence `defence` (`agl + level`). A hit rolls the weapon's damage (`damageRoll`, of the range, `min` and `max`, and two rolls from 0 up to 1, `roll` and `roll2`: by default uniform, `min + floor(roll × (max − min + 1))`; also for breaking solids) plus `damageBonus` (`(str − 10) / 2`, never below 0), less the target's armor after the attacker's penetration (the weapon's plus `penetrationBonus`, `(str − 10) / 4`, if more than 0), which can't go below 0: armor can soak a hit completely. A miss shows "miss", a soaked hit "0". No criticals or fumbles yet. The rolls come from the game's seed, in turn (`Game.combatRolls`, saved), so a game plays the same again. All the formulas are **rules** (expr attrs of level and the stats, rounded down), so a map can change them.
- **Tuning** (2026-11-24): the combat and action-point rules' defaults are in `data/rules.json5`'s `rules` (with what each does), read when the index loads (`settings.setDefaultRules`), over those in `RULES`, which are only fallbacks; a map's `rules` go over them, for that map.
- **Max HP**: the profile's `hp` if fixed (enemies: a goblin 5, a creeper 4), else `maxHp` (`10 + con / 2 + (level − 1) × (2 + con / 5)`: 15 at level 1 with con 10).
- **XP and levels**: the piece that kills enemies gets their xp; at `xpToLevel` (`20 × level`) it goes up a level, and its max HP with it (HP up by as much). Levelling is automatic, but each level up gives **stat points** to spend (see Stat points). XP is kept in **hundredths** (`XP_UNIT`, 2026-11-26), as action points are, so small shares add up; it shows as points (12.35/20).
- **Stat points** (2026-11-26, `Combat.spendStatPoints`): each level up gives the piece **`statPointsPerLevel`** (2; a formula of the new level and stats) points; `piece.statPoints` is `{available, spent}` (`spent`: all it's ever spent). Spending puts points into stats (only up: never below what they were), and max HP and HP follow (`updateMaxHp`, as at a level up). `withStats(p, adds)` is the piece as it would be, for the sheet's preview. Stat limits (min, max) are left for species, much later.
- **Level up, shown** (2026-11-26): rings (`Render.drawLevelUp`, three, 0.15 apart, over `LEVEL_UP_MS` 1400 ms) grow from the piece's selection ring to past its cell, easing out, thinning and fading from `--level-up` (gold) to white, with "Level n!" (both after the "+n XP" has risen). A piece of yours with points to spend has an **arrow up** (`circle-arrow-up`, in `--level-up`) first among its status icons, and the piece menu's Character item **pulses** (`.hc-Btn.attention`; PieceMenu items may have `attention()`) while the selected piece has points.
- **Whose kill** (2026-11-26, `Game.damage`): an enemy remembers the last of your pieces to hurt it (`lastHitBy`: the attacker, or the piece that set off a harm that hit it), and that piece gets its xp however it dies (with the xp of what was linked below it), if it's still there. The xp is given once the action is over (`Game.pendingKills`, given by `Combat.awardKills` in `afterPlayerAction`, and at the end of your turn).
- **Multi-kills** (2026-11-26, `Combat.awardKills`): the kills of one action (a move or attack, a group attack with several of your pieces, pieces destroyed below a killed leader, what a harm you set off kills) are one multi-kill of `killCount` enemy pieces. Each of your pieces that killed some gets **`multiKillBonusXP`** of their xp (`xp + xp * Math.min(20, killCount - 1) / 20`: 5% more for each kill after the first, at most double; of `xp` in points, `killCount` and `level`), before XP sharing. Two or more show "n× multi-kill!" over the first killer and a log line. It replaced the score combo (gone with score on 2026-11-25).
- **XP sharing** (2026-11-26, `Combat.awardXp`): the killer gets the kill's xp; if it has a parent it first gets **`childXPBonus`** more (`xp * parentRank / 20`: 5% a parent rank), then passes **`parentXPShare`** of all it got (`xp * rank / 20`, of its own rank: 5% a rank) up to its parent, which does the same with what it got (its own bonus from its parent, then a share up), and so on to the top. The variables of both: `xp` (in points), `rank` (the piece's), `parentRank` and `level`. Each piece that gains shows "+n XP" and a log line. Rank now matters: a Tree swarm shares more than a Snake (rank 1 everywhere).
- **Solids** don't dodge: an attack on one just rolls damage, less its DR after penetration; a piece can attack one only if its most damage could get through.
- **The combat log** (`Game.combatLog`, `logCombat`): entries `{turn, kind, ...}` for hits (`roll`, `chance`, `dmg`), soaked hits, misses, harm damage, pieces destroyed (`linked`), XP and level-ups, with who as `whoOf` gives it (`{owner, core, enemyId}`), so an entry reads the same once the piece is gone. The view words them (see UI).
- Level replaces rank wherever rank stood for strength: costs, spawn pressure, the caverns' challenge (`level`: your highest; `levels`: your pieces' levels together), goals (`coreLevel`, `maxLevel`, `totalLevels`), and the symbol inside a piece.

## Action points
(2026-11-24, `model/Combat.js`; they replace moves per turn, Recover, Quicken and a jump's tiredness.)
- Every piece has **action points** (`piece.ap`, saved), kept in hundredths (`AP_UNIT` 100 a point) as whole numbers so they add up exactly. An action costs `actionCost` (**1**): a step or attack, a swap (each of the two pieces), a detach or an attach, and a jump (until 2026-11-25 a jump cost `jumpCost`, 2; now it costs an action and exhausts the piece: see Status effects). A **step onto a floor tile** (see Quadrants and tiles) costs its `moveCost` times an action (`Combat.moveCost`, 2026-11-24): for a group, the dearest cell any piece of it lands on (it goes at its slowest), but never more than the piece's whole pool, so dear going is slow but never a wall (a piece needs its pool full to cross it then). Attacks, swaps and jumps don't pay it; advancing after a kill stays free. Your pieces' moves are only offered when they can pay (`legalMoves`). Both are rules of level and stats, so per piece, and at least 0.1 (`MIN_COST`). A piece acts only with the points for it, and may act again while it has them. Pieces carried by a move spend nothing.
- **Pool** `apPool` (`1 + con / 10`: 2 with con 10): the most a piece can have, but never less than 1, or an action's cost, whatever the rule gives, so everything can act. **Recovery** `apRecovery` (`0.5 + agl / 40`: 0.75 with agl 10): what every piece gets back at the end of each turn, after the enemies', up to its pool. **Initiative** `apInitiative` (`(wis + agl) / 40`: 0.5 with 10s): when an enemy first comes into your sight (`piece.noticed`) it gets its initiative's points, and your pieces get theirs, once a turn (`Game.initiativeTurn`), checked after each of your actions and after the enemies' turn; up to their pools. The rules are formulas of level and the stats, not rounded.
- A new piece starts with **0**; your core starts a game with 1.
- Shown as a thin bar under the HP bar, with a tick at each whole point, and as "AP" in the info panel (2026-11-25: no longer in the footer too) (the selected piece's, of its pool). A piece of yours without the points to act is drawn inverted.
- Squads plan with their pieces' points (`planSquad`: the search goes as deep as the actions they have the points for), and Go to, Explore and the formations use the swarm's (`planUntangle` gives the swarm its recovery when none can act, but not an exhausted piece, and counts a jump's exhaustion, the recovery it loses, as points spent, so it walks where a jump saves nothing).

## Status effects
(2026-11-25, `model/Effects.js`, a module included in Game.)
- A piece has a list of **status effects** (`piece.effects`, `[{id, countdown}]`, saved). Their kinds are environs.json5's `effects` table (`name`, `description`, to show; `extends` works); what each does is the game's, by its id.
- **Duration:** `countdown`, the turns it lasts. At the end of each turn, in housekeeping, after action points come back, every countdown goes down by 1 and an effect ends at 0 (that order, so an effect for 1 turn still counts at the end of the turn it was got in). Getting an effect a piece already has **adds** to its countdown. Other ways to end (a save against it, say: saves aren't built yet) would be other properties beside `id`.
- **Shown or not** (2026-11-25): an effect kind may be `"muted":true` (no icon, but it's in the character sheet and the enemy hover text: for long-lasting things, curses, diseases, crippling wounds) or `"hidden":true` (shown nowhere: the game's own bookkeeping).
- **Rallied** (muted): can't rally (see Rally), for `rallyRefresh` turns.
- **Rallying** (hidden): gets back `rallyAmount` HP at the end of each turn (`rallyHeal`), and can't rally (see Rally).
- **Exhausted:** the piece gets no action points back at the end of the turn (`recoverAp`); initiative still gives them. A **jump** exhausts a piece for `jumpExhaustion` turns (a rule of level and stats, **2**; 0 for none), each jump adding as many. With stats of 10 a jump so costs an action plus two turns' 0.75, about 2.5 points, and every piece can afford one (goblins, pool 1.8, couldn't jump for 2 points; the AI's field without jumps for them is gone).
- Shown in the character sheet (Status: "Exhausted (1 more turn)", or "Fine") and after a named enemy's description when you point at it. **Icons** (2026-11-25, `Render.js` `drawEffects`): above each piece, as far out as its bars are below it, a small icon for each effect (a fifth of a cell, at least 8 px), left to right from the left side of its hex (so they stand out, and there's room for several), white (`--effect-icon`), with nothing behind it. An effect kind names its icon in environs.json5 (`"icon"`, one of `img/icons`, Lucide's: exhausted is `battery-low`); it's loaded once, the first time it's needed, and turned into a Path2D to stroke (`svgPath`: path, circle, ellipse, line, rect, polyline and polygon), and the board is drawn again when it arrives. Next: blinded, stunned, paralyzed..., most with countdowns.

## Actions that cost gold
- **Place piece** (P): costs `placeCost` (`4 + ceil(levels / 2)`: levels, your pieces' levels together). From the selected piece's menu (or P): choose an empty cell next to the selected piece (not a harm tile you know of); the new piece is linked to it (2026-11-21; before, any cell next to any piece, then the piece to link to). New pieces start at full HP with no action points.
- **Rally** (R, 2026-11-25, `Combat.playerRally`; it replaced Heal, which spent score: `healCost` 5 × core level for `healAmount` 4 × core level HP, flowing along links): a piece of yours that's hurt and isn't rallying spends `rallyCost` action points (**0.5**, at least `MIN_COST`) and is **rallying** (a hidden status effect: see Status effects) for `rallyTurns` turns (**5** since 2026-11-27; it was 3; at least 1). At the end of each of them (housekeeping, after action points come back and before the countdowns) it gets back `rallyAmount` HP (**(con / 10) × level**: 1 a turn with con 10 at level 1), up to its max, only itself (nothing flows along links). It can't rally again until that's over, nor for `rallyRefresh` turns from when it rallied (2026-11-25: **Math.max(10, 150 - level)**; a muted effect, **rallied**, so the character sheet shows how long). All four are rules of level and stats. A piece that could rally keeps the turn from ending by itself.

## The swarm
- **Movement modes** (2026-11-20, `model/Movement.js`): how the pieces linked to a piece move with it. **Fixed**: the whole subtree moves by the same step, keeping its shape (as it always did). **Free**: only the piece moves; a piece below it that would end out of link range is dragged by the same step (and so on down), and if a dragged piece can't move there, neither can the piece. **Follow** (for leading a swarm through tight places): the piece leads and its **whole tree** follows: what's below it, and its parent, grandparent and so on with their other branches (a detached group follows its own top piece). Followers go **in footsteps** (2026-11-22; they kept their places relative to each other before, which left a piece whose link was stretched caught on wall corners, stopping the whole swarm): each piece remembers the cells it was on, latest first, as many as the link range (`piece.trail`, saved with the piece; a jump starts it afresh). A Follow step is a **search** (2026-11-23; taking the best cell for each piece in turn failed where an early piece's choice blocked a later one, as when a piece is behind its own children in a tunnel): the lead goes to its cell; each other piece stays or moves a cell, so that no two end on the same cell, none on a piece outside the swarm, none onto a harm tile you know of (armed or not; it may stay on one), no two followers trade cells (a piece the lead steps onto may take the lead's), and no link ends out of range unless it got shorter. Working out from the lead along links, each piece tries first the cells it would rather take: keeping its link to the piece it was reached from (its guide) in range, then the furthest along the guide's footsteps (the cell it just left, then its trail), never back along them (off them, the nearest the latest of them), then staying put; and if that leaves no way on for a piece after it (or a piece linked to it couldn't get back in range), the next. It gives up after 3,000 tries (`FOLLOW_TRIES`). So a swarm files after its leader through tight places and round corners; in the open it strings out behind it rather than keeping its shape. A step that can't be done in Follow is tried in Fixed, then Free (2026-11-23), unless that would make it an attack. In every mode no link may end out of range unless it got shorter. **Attacks:** in Fixed, every piece of the subtree that would land on an enemy (or a solid it can break) hits it; in Free and Follow only the piece itself attacks, and the others go round. You pick the mode for your moves with **Move: …** in the footer (M cycles it): Fixed or Follow (`PLAYER_MOVE_MODES`; Free was one too until 2026-11-23, when shift-click took it over, so a saved game or map asking for it starts in Fixed); it's saved with the game (`Game.moveMode`), and new games start in the `moveMode` *(setting, Fixed)*. Enemies: a far-off squad marches in Fixed if that gets it closer, else Follow, then Free; near you a squad plans with Fixed and Free only (one result kept where they agree), since Follow would let one piece bring the whole squad in and still leave the leader's group attack, which beat striking and pulling back. `resolveStep(view, id, dx, dy, mode)` holds the rules for player and enemies alike, on a view of the board (`boardView`) or of positions the planner is trying.
- Moving a piece moves its subtree as its mode says (in Fixed, all of it by the same step). Only the chosen piece spends a move, so pieces it carries can still move themselves afterward. Order matters: reshape the children first, then carry the formation with the parent, or the other way round.
  - Group attack (Fixed): if any member would step onto an enemy, every such member hits the enemy in front of it (then the advance rule applies). In Free and Follow only the chosen piece attacks.
  - Otherwise every member must land on an empty cell or one vacated by the group.
- Link range: a linked piece must end within 3 steps of its parent *(setting)*. The root is unrestricted.
- When a piece is destroyed, every descendant is destroyed too (both sides).
- **Use** (U, 2026-11-25): see Features (doors and other fixtures' actions).
- **Attack** (F, since A moves west; 2026-11-25, `Game.playerAttack`): the selected piece attacks a cell next to it (the six around it), for an action, without stepping: an enemy piece, a solid it can break, or a fixture you know of with HP whose DR it can beat (`canBreakFixture`), **in the way or not** (a step only attacks fixtures in the way, so this is how you hit one you could walk onto, like a broken barricade). Only that piece attacks (not what's linked below it, as a step's group attack does), and it doesn't advance after. In the piece menu (Attack) or F: the cells it can attack are highlighted (`--attack`), a click attacks, Esc cancels; nothing it can't damage is highlighted. Hits go through `applyHits`, shared with a step's attack (kills, XP, breaking). A piece that could attack keeps the turn from ending by itself. Enemies don't attack fixtures yet.
- **Jump** (J, 2026-11-21, `model/Movement.js`): a piece moves two cells: to any of the twelve cells two steps away, straight over one cell or between two. It can jump over holes and pieces (either side's), but not over a solid: the cell it passes over, or one of the two, must not be solid. It must land on a cell it can stand on (no solid, hole or piece; a player piece may land on harm, an enemy won't), and lands on that cell's tiles. Nothing linked to it moves, so every link of it must end in range (no "got shorter" allowance). It costs **an action** and exhausts the piece for a turn (see Status effects; until 2026-11-25 it cost 2 action points, and until 2026-11-24 it left the piece tired, unable to act that turn or the next). Not in Follow mode. Enemies jump too (2026-11-21), to get at you, but only over gaps: cells they couldn't stand on (holes, or harm), where walking would take the long way round; the planner charges a jump `AI.jump` for its exhaustion. The distance field counts a jump as 2 actions (an action and about as much again in lost recovery), and those jumps (see below), so a jump is their way forward when it beats walking round. The planner tries every such jump of each piece with the points (after its steps, so a step wins a tie), except a piece next to one of yours (`AI.jumpClose`, 1), where the points are better spent striking and pulling back; each costs `AI.jump` (0.5, a step's worth of approach) in its score, so in open ground it walks. A marching squad jumps one of its pieces when no step gets it closer: the jump that gets its nearest piece closest, or else its pieces closer in all, so the rest follow the first over. A squad whose leader hasn't the points doesn't step, but its other pieces may jump. **By clicking** (2026-11-23, `V.jumpAt`): with a piece selected and no mode on, hovering over a cell it can jump to highlights it and draws the piece there as a ghost (and colours a harm, heal or loot cell it would land on, as for a move), and a click jumps (the cells a jump goes to are two away, a move's one, so they never clash); Jump in the menu (J) still shows them all, now with the ghost too.
- **Go to and Explore** (G, O, 2026-11-22, `model/Navigation.js`, App): the selected piece goes somewhere by itself, to save tedious moves (backtracking above all). **Go to** asks for a cell, as Place does; **Explore** heads for the nearest cell you've seen that's next to one you haven't, again and again. Either way it goes stepping while it has the action points, in Follow mode whatever the movement mode (`playerAct` takes a mode), so its swarm trails after it, and ends the turn when it hasn't (your other pieces' points that turn go unused); the next step comes once that turn has played (`animationEnd`), or after a move's time with animation off. A step that leaves the piece without the points for another ends the turn with it, and (2026-11-24) every turn takes at least a **beat** (a step's time, at the Animation speed), even one where nothing you can see happens: so the pace doesn't change with enemies out of sight, and you see the piece's points come back on the turns it can't step. The route is planned again before every step, by a search out from the piece (plain steps, no jumps; up to 120, a step onto a cell you haven't seen counting 3): on cells you've seen that are open, with no harm tile you know of and no piece on them but one of its own swarm (counting 2, since it makes way in Follow), and for Go to through cells you haven't seen too, so you can send it into the fog (Explore keeps to what you've seen). If the step can't be done (no arrangement of its swarm lets it), it **untangles** its swarm (2026-11-23, `planUntangle`): a search, best first, over positions of the swarm (up to 400), from actions each piece of it can take (once a turn, as many as the moves a turn allow): a Free step, a swap with a piece next to it, a jump (counting 3 actions, as it tires the piece), and for the lead a Follow step, for the fewest actions that get the lead nearer where it's going, preferring slack links on the way (each link's length past 1 counts, and a step nearer for the lead counts 3). How near is a map of the cost from each cell (`goalField`: out from the piece to the goals within 12 of the nearest, then back from them, with its swarm out of the way). It takes the plan's first action, then plans again, using the swarm's action points while untangling, and ending the turn when the next action is in a turn to come; it keeps off positions its swarm has been in since it set off, so it can't go round in circles. (Before this, a stuck step tried other steps no further from the goal, then a swap, then a jump; the search does all of that and more.) It stops, saying why on the status line: on getting there, when there's no way on (or nothing left to explore within reach), when one of your pieces is hurt (even by a trap you didn't know of), when an enemy comes into view that wasn't in sight the step before, and at any key or click on the board (which does nothing else). If it stops after its own step, the turn stays yours. Why it stopped shows until a key, a click or pointing at another cell, when the status line goes back to saying what's under the pointer.
- **Swap** (S, 2026-11-21): two pieces of the same side next to each other swap cells (any two, linked or not). Their links stay with them, and nothing else moves; it's allowed only if every link of either ends in range, or got shorter. It uses one move, and both pieces are done for the turn. Each lands on the other's cell's tiles. Enemies can swap too: the planner considers swapping any two squad pieces that haven't acted, though its scoring rarely has a reason to (it doesn't value which piece is where). **By Alt-clicking** (2026-11-23, `V.swapAt`; Option on a Mac): with a piece selected, holding Alt highlights the pieces it can swap with, and over one shows the two as ghosts in each other's cells (as Swap in the menu now does too); Alt-click swaps. A plain click on one of your pieces still selects it.
- **Detach and attach** (X and L, 2026-11-20): a piece other than the core can be **detached** from its parent, however far apart they are, keeping what's linked below it; a piece with no parent (not the core) can be **attached** to any adjacent piece of yours, in the core's group or another detached one, but not one linked below it (no loops). Either is an action, for that piece's points; ranks are recomputed. A detached group acts like any other: it moves and attacks, carrying what's below it, and isn't held by link range. Enemies can't detach or attach. Later piece types may not allow either.
- **Formations** (N and T, 2026-11-23, `model/Formation.js`, App): **Snake** and **Tree** in the piece menu get the selected piece's swarm (its whole tree) into a shape, by real actions at their usual costs: detaching, walking (a group cut loose steps in Follow, its top leading, toward a free cell next to where it goes) and attaching. Like Go to, they run by themselves, using the swarm's action points, ending the turn when nothing more can be done in it, and stop at a key or click, when an enemy comes into view or a piece is hurt, when done ("In formation."), or when they can't get on (no way to a free cell next to where a group goes). Starting again on the same swarm takes up the groups the last one cut loose. The rest of the swarm stays put meanwhile. **Snake**: a single chain from the top (the core) to the selected piece, cut loose first and attached last so it's the free end, to lead: a piece with more than one child keeps the one with most below it; each group cut loose is walked to the end of the chain and attached there. **Tree**: as near a complete binary tree from the top as it can get: extra children (past two) are cut loose; groups go to the shallowest piece with a free place (fewer than two children), the nearest it can get to first; then the deepest piece without children moves to a free place two or more levels nearer the top, until there's none. Why: in a tunnel a step wide the pieces are in a line, so a swarm of n pieces whose longest chain has D links can't fit if (n − 1) / D > the link range (a complete binary tree of height h fits only if ⌈(2^h − 1) / h⌉ ≤ the range: with 3, 15 pieces fit and 31 don't), while a snake always fits. The trade-off is rank (1 + a piece's second-highest child rank): a snake has the lowest everywhere, a tree the highest for its size (rank sets XP sharing).

## Map components
(2026-11-21, `model/map/MapComponent.js`, `model/map/Generators.js`, `model/map/enemy_ai/EnemyAI.js`.) A map's tiles come from its **tile generator**, its enemies from its **spawn generator**, and their turns from its **enemy AI** (2026-11-22). The base classes `hc.TileGenerator`, `hc.SpawnGenerator` and `hc.EnemyAI` say what the game calls and when; a component belongs to one game (`game.tileGenerator`, `game.spawnGenerator`, `game.enemyAI`), keeps whatever else it needs as its own, and is saved with the game as `{type, attrs, state}` (`exportState` / `importState`). Each type registers with its base class (`TileGenerator.register(name, Type)`, a class method, as are `has(name)`, `make(name, game, attrs)`, `cleanAttrs(name, attrs, where)` and the `types` registry; the shared ones are on `hc.MapComponent`, which was `hc.Generator` until the enemy AI joined them).
- **Attrs** (2026-11-22): a component class declares the attrs it takes, with their defaults and ranges, in its `ATTRS` class attribute (with `model/Attrs.js`: `num`, `bool`, `choice`; a `num` can have a step below 1, e.g. 0.1); a map gives values for them (see Maps), and a component has all of them in `this.attrs`. The values are checked like the rules: numbers rounded and clamped, choices from their options, anything else skipped with a warning saying where.
- **TileGenerator:** `start()` (a new game, the core in place), `piecesMoved()` (your pieces moved or one was placed; then the game reveals tiles).
- **SpawnGenerator:** `start()` (a new game: the starting enemies), `turnEnded()` (each new turn: waves).
- **EnemyAI:** `takeTurn()` (the enemies' turn). Game's `enemyPhase` calls it and animates what it does: the AI sets `game.track` to the squad acting and calls `game.endAction()` after each action.
- The tile generators are **"proximity"**: `ProximityTileGenerator` (the map made around your pieces as they go, by quadrant: see Quadrants and tiles; it keeps the quadrants made, borders and quadrants visited, which the `quadsVisited` goal value and the visited tint read), and **"caverns"** (2026-11-22): `CavernsTileGenerator` (caverns joined by tunnels in solid rock: see Caverns). The spawn generators are **"proximity"**: `ProximitySpawnGenerator` (waves around your core: see Enemy pressure and Enemy pathing and spawning), and **"caverns"** (2026-11-24): `CavernsSpawnGenerator` (bands waiting in caverns and tunnels: see Caverns spawning). The enemy AI is **"squad"**: `SquadAI` (see Enemy AI). Their attrs are below (Settings).
- An attr can be a **table** (2026-11-22): weights for some of a set of subtypes, `{subtype:weight}` (whole numbers, 0 to 1000), from which one is picked at random in proportion (`attrs.pick`; all 0 picks none). A table with an unknown subtype or a bad weight is skipped with a warning, like any attr.

## Named enemies
- **Enemies and groups** (2026-11-23, `model/Enemies.js`, data in `data/entities.json5`): `enemies` says what a single enemy piece is, by id (`name`, `description`; attributes to come: every piece of one is a `core` for now, its rank from how it's linked). `enemyGroups` are enemies as they come, linked into trees (`name`, `description`, `members`): members is a list of nodes, each an enemy's id (one piece, alone), `{"<enemy id>":[nodes]}` for an enemy with those linked below it, or a group's id standing for all its trees, so groups build on groups (a goblin pack is a goblin reaver with three goblin trios below it, each a reaver leading two goblins: 10 pieces, rank 3). A group can't have nodes below it (which of its pieces would they hang from?); an id names an enemy or a group, not both; a group that breaks a rule, names something unknown or is in itself is left out with a console error. (The notation is arrays of nodes, not objects, since siblings of the same type would be duplicate keys.)
- **Extending** (2026-11-24, `model/tile/Subtypes.js`): any entry of a data table (in `init.json5` or a file it includes) (materials, solids, holes, fixtures, characters, enemies; not enemy groups, which are only their members) can say `"extends":"<id>"` of another in the same table: it's that one with its own properties over it, objects (like `stats`) merged a property at a time all the way down, anything else (text, numbers, arrays like `damage`) replaced. Chains of any length work. It's worked out once, when the table is defined, before each entry is checked (so a child can complete a parent that wouldn't do on its own); an entry whose parent isn't there, is bad, or is in a loop with it is left out with an error saying why. The **goblin reaver** extends the goblin (str and con 12, damage 2–6, 8 HP, 10 xp).
- **Spawning one** (`SpawnGenerator.spawnGroup(id, spots)`): tries the spots in turn; at the first it fits, its first tree's top piece goes there, each piece below on a free cell next to the one it's linked to, each other tree's top piece on the nearest free cell within 3 of the first (free: open, empty, no harm tile, 2 or more from your pieces). If it fits nowhere, nothing spawns. Its pieces carry the enemy's id (`piece.enemyId`) and a **band** they share (`piece.band`, from `Game.newBand`), saved with them, so the AI can tell they belong together (how a band's trees work together is still to come: each tree is its own squad for now). A spawn generator's table can name an enemy (one alone) or a group. Hovering over a named enemy you can see shows its name and description.

## Enemy pressure (tied to player level)
(The "proximity" spawn generator; tied to rank until 2026-11-24.)
- **L = your highest piece level.** The turn count doesn't drive difficulty (an optional clock ramp exists, off by default).
- A wave arrives every 3 turns with **base + perLevel × (L − 1)** enemies = **1, 3, 5, 7, 9…** at level 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. They're creepers.
- The wave is split into squads of the **squad cap or one less**, where squad cap = **L + 1** (max 8).
- 3 single enemies at the start.

## Enemy squads
- Squads are link trees shaped as binary trees (piece i links to piece ⌊(i−1)/2⌋), so a squad of 3 has a rank-2 leader, 7 a rank-3 leader.
- A squad's pieces act with their own **action points**, as yours do (see Action points). Moving a squad piece carries everything linked below it, and group attacks work the same as the player's.
- Killing a squad's leader destroys the whole squad, and every destroyed piece counts as a kill (and gives its XP).

## Enemy AI (squad planning)
The "squad" enemy AI (`model/map/enemy_ai/SquadAI.js`); its numbers are its attrs (see Settings).
- **Far squads** (nearest piece more than 6 steps from yours, by the distance field) march: the whole squad steps one cell along the shortest path that avoids harm tiles, keeping its shape, or, if no step gets it closer, one of its pieces jumps (see Jump).
- **Near squads** plan their turn with a **beam search** (12 plans kept per step) over sequences of single-piece actions, up to their action budget. A plan is scored on:
  - expected damage (2026-11-25; until then every hit was a sure 1 damage): +10 per HP a strike can be expected to take (its hit chance × its damage odds, `Combat.damageOdds`, capped by the HP left), +15 more per HP on the core, and +6 per player piece destroyed (including pieces linked below it) and +1000 for the core, each times the chance of it. A plan keeps the odds of each struck piece's HP, so a second strike on the same piece is worth what it can still take. A piece the plan is sure to have destroyed is gone (its cell can be stepped into); one it might have is still in the way. So a goblin's swing at the adventurer (44%, 0–3) is worth about 6.6 and a reaver's (56%, 3–7) about 28: goblins hang back until patience runs out, reavers press;
  - exposure at the end of the turn: −**caution** (12, *setting*) × (pieces in its subtree) for every squad piece left next to a player piece (×0.6 if it has more than 1 HP), times the chance one of those player pieces is still there;
  - position: +1 for each piece ending exactly 2 steps away (can strike next turn, can't be hit), −0.5 per step for pieces further out.
  - While searching, a partial plan also gets credit for strikes it has set up but not made yet (each piece's best strike's expected damage, up to the actions left), so "carry into reach → strike → pull back with an ancestor" isn't pruned at its first, bad-looking step.
- **Stale plans** (2026-11-25): a plan is made before any dice are rolled, so it can go stale: a strike kills its target, and the next attack planned there would now be a step into the empty cell. When an action no longer does what was planned (an attack is now a step), the squad plans the rest of its turn again, from there, with the points left (but not twice in a row without acting).
- **Patience** (2 turns, *setting*): each turn a nearby squad goes without landing a hit, its caution drops (to 0 after 2 idle turns), so it can't hang back at a safe distance forever. A hit resets it. Without patience, cautious squads hovered 2 steps away indefinitely.
- Observed against a wall of player pieces: a leader moves the squad in for a 6-hit group strike, then the mid pieces pull their branches back; or a mid piece strikes and the leader pulls everything back, with idle turns at a safe distance in between. Against a lone, surrounded target there is no safe retreat (any step back carries a piece onto the target, which counts as another attack), so squads keep attacking.

## Quadrants and tiles
- The "proximity" tile generator divides the world into **quadrants** (hexagonal chunks, `tileGenerator.quads`). They're a gameplay mechanic with no lines of their own (2026-11-21); only the visited tint shows them. The start cell is the centre of quadrant (0,0).
- When a player piece is in a quadrant, every quadrant within ⌈vision radius ÷ (3 × quadrant radius + 2)⌉ quadrants of it (at least 1, which it is with the defaults) gets its **solid tiles**, so they exist wherever you could see; this doesn't depend on whether fog of war is on. **Heal, harm and loot tiles** are placed only in that quadrant and its 6 neighbours (a holdover from when they had countdowns from when they were made). Each is **once per quadrant**, never cleaned up. Visited quadrants are tinted light blue.
- **Border walls** make chambers: when a quadrant's terrain is made, each of its borders not yet decided becomes a wall with a 50% chance *(setting)*. A wall is tiles on every cell of the quadrant touching that neighbour, except a **door** of 1–2 cells *(settings; 0 makes a solid wall)* in the middle, never at a corner, where walls meet. 25% of walls *(setting)* are holes rather than solid tiles: they block movement but not sight. Each border is decided from the seed (see Maps) and the border alone, and the wall sits on the side of the quadrant whose key comes first; decisions are kept in `Game.borders` (saved). **No quadrant is ever walled in on all six sides:** if all six of a quadrant's borders would be walls with no door, the first of them (by key) gets a door of 1. Random clumps can still block a door. Walls go in first; the random clumps below come after, so holes can replace part of a wall.
- Each quadrant gets **1 heal tile and 1 harm tile** *(settings)* at random empty cells, **0–3 solid tiles** *(settings)* in one connected clump (the first on a random free cell, each next one on a free cell of the quadrant touching the clump), and then **0–3 hole tiles** *(settings)* in a clump the same way. Holes may land on solid tiles and replace them, so they can break up a solid clump.
- **Floor tiles** (2026-11-24, `data/environs.json5`'s `floors`, `model/tile/Solids.js`): open floor that's harder going, like **loose stone** (1.5), **mud** (2) and **ice** (1.5). A floor blocks neither sight nor movement, and takes fixtures, but a step onto it costs its `moveCost` (default 1, at least 0.1) times an action (see Action points). It's drawn in its `fillColor` under the shadows, and floors of a kind run together, with no grid lines between them; pointing at one, or at plain ground (its map's base floor), says what it is and, if it isn't 1, its cost. The enemies' distance field and Go to / Explore's routes count it (in tenths of a step), so they go round dear going when that's cheaper. **Base floors** (cost 1): **stone floor**, **cave floor**, **dirt floor** and **frozen floor**. A cell with no tile is its map's base floor (the tile generator's `baseFloor`: stone floor for proximity, cave floor for caverns; the Hedge Maze has dirt, the ice caverns frozen floor), which colours the known ground and sets what a step onto it costs (`Game.baseFloor`, `moveCostAt`). **Placed** by the proximity generator as one clump a quadrant (`floorMin` / `floorMax` of `floorType`, 0 / 0; Endless Play has up to 3 of loose stone), before solids and holes, which may land on it (it has its own generator, so the rest come out the same); and by the caverns generator as round **patches** anywhere open (`floorFrequency` per 1000 cells, 8, each of radius `floorSizeMin` to `floorSizeMax`, 0 to 2, from `floorTable`, loose stone 2, mud 1; the ice caverns ice 3, loose stone 1), worked out cell by cell from the seed (the nearest patch reaching a cell, `floorPatchAt` / `floorOf`), so blocks come out the same in any order. Stalagmites, stalactites and holes go over them, and heals, harms and loot can sit on them.
- **Tiles and fixtures** (2026-11-24, `model/tile/Fixtures.js`): a cell has a **tile**, its terrain (`Game.tiles`: a solid, a hole or a floor, or plain floor where there's none), and at most one **fixture** over it (`Game.fixtures`), only on open floor: something that sits on the ground and does something when one of your pieces enters it. Heals, harms and loot are fixtures (until then they were tiles too, so a cell couldn't be both). Fixtures are plain data, saved in the game's `fixtures`. The generators place them where there's no tile, fixture or piece; terrain never goes on a fixture.
- **Kinds of fixture** (2026-11-21 as heal, harm and loot subtypes): `data/environs.json5`'s `"fixtures"` lists them (`extends` works), each with a **type**, `"heal"`, `"harm"` or `"loot"`, which the rest of the game goes by (pieces keep off harms, the generators' `healType`, `harmType` and `lootType` *(settings)* and tables list only kinds of that type); a heal is loot instead at **10%** *(setting)*. Besides a name and description, a kind says:
  - **revealMode**: when the tile shows: `"instant"` (default: once its cell is in sight; at once with fog of war off), `"hidden"` (once a piece of yours enters it) or `{"proximity":n}` (once a piece of yours is within n cells, in sight or not). Once revealed it stays revealed.
  - **pool**: what it holds (HP, damage or gold): n, `{"min","max"}` or `"infinite"`.
  - **magnitude**: what it gives each piece it affects, each use: n, `{"min","max"}` or `"pool"` (default: all that's left; not with an infinite pool). Never more than is left. Ranges are rolled when the tile is made, from the seed (their own generator, so the cells don't change).
  - **showMagnitude**: when its magnitude shows, as revealMode (`"hidden"`: once it's used). **showPool** (default false): whether what's left shows.
  - **area**: which pieces it affects when one of yours enters it: a radius (default 0) or `{"radius", "shape"}`: `flat` (all within the radius), `ring` (only exactly the radius away, so not the piece on it) or `fade` (magnitude × (radius + 1 − distance) / (radius + 1), rounded). Nearest first (then by id), so the pool can run out part way.
  - **duration**: when it's gone: `"exhausted"` (default: its pool is empty, so never with an infinite pool), `"once"`, `{"count":n}` (after n uses) or `{"countdown":n}` (n turns after it's **revealed**, so tiles you haven't found don't run out). A tile is always gone once its pool is empty.
- **Features** (2026-11-25): a fixture of type `feature` does nothing when entered (no pool or magnitude), but every fixture kind may now have what a solid has, each optional:
  - `state` (`{"open":false}`: true, false or numbers, copied onto each fixture and saved), whose names, with `hp` (what it has left, or 0), its formulas can use (see Settings for the formula language: comparisons, `&& || !` and `c ? a : b` were added for this);
  - `composition` (a material: its hardness is the fixture's DR, its opacity times `coverage` how much it blocks sight), `hp` (with it, it can be attacked as a solid is, when it's in the way: only your pieces, only if their most damage beats its DR; it keeps its own HP, saved), `coverage` (0–100, default 100, or a formula: a door shut or open), `floor` (the floor laid under it when it's gone);
  - `moveCost` (default 1, or a formula): a step onto it costs that **times the floor's** (`Game.moveCostAt`), so a group pays the dearest as before; **0 or less** and it's **in the way**, as a solid is (`fixtureBlocks`, checked now, so a door opening takes effect at once): `Game.isOpen` is false there, so nothing steps onto it, is placed there or lands there, the enemies and Go to path round it, and nothing jumps over it (`blocksJumps`, also the enemies' field's). One in the way shows once it's in sight, whatever its `revealMode` (you're never stopped by something you can't see);
  - `destroyInto` (a fixture id): what it leaves in its place **whenever it's gone** (broken, used up, or its countdown run out: `removeFixture`), revealed if it was; a loot chest could leave an open one;
  - `fillColor`, `borderColor`: a feature is drawn as a hexagon inside its cell, in these (else a solid's colours), faint when it isn't in the way. Pointing at one with HP says what it is and how tough, as for a solid, and once damaged it has a HP bar across its middle, as a solid has (`drawDamage`); a step's cost on its floor includes its `moveCost`.
  - environs.json5 has a **Barricade** (pine, 10 HP, 80% coverage, in the way) that leaves a **Broken barricade** (passable, 1.5×, 20% coverage), which leaves **Wood scraps**. Both tile generators place features (2026-11-25): `featureFrequency` per 1000 cells (5), of the kinds in `featureTable` (barricade), with their own random numbers after the other fixtures, so those come out where they were. The caverns put them only on cavern floor off the tunnels' paths (where stalagmites go), so none cuts a cavern off; the proximity generator on any free cell but the start and next to it (one may stand in a gap in a wall: you can break it, and the enemies go round). 
- **Facing and actions** (2026-11-25): a kind may be **`orientable`**: each one has a `facing` (0–5, an index into `hex.dirs`, saved; random when made, unless its generator sets it), which splits the six cells round it (`fixtureSide`): **front** (directions `facing` and `facing + 1`), **sides** (`+ 2` and `+ 5`, opposite each other: the wall a door is in) and **back** (`+ 3` and `+ 4`). A kind's **`actions`** (`{name:{from, when, set, onStep, label}}`) are what a piece of yours next to it can do to it, for an action: `from` the sides it can be done from (default all; a fixture that isn't orientable: any), `when` a formula of its state (it can be done only when this isn't 0), `set` new values for its state (numbers, true/false, or formulas of its state as it was; a true/false one stays one), `onStep` true for what a step into it does when it's in the way (the piece stays put, and the step does nothing else: no attack), `label` its name in the UI. An action can't put a fixture in the way of a piece standing on it (a door won't shut on you). `fixtureActions(p, f)` lists what p can do now; `playerUse` does one (and resets sight, so an opened door shows what's behind it at once); `stepAction` is what a step does. Entering a door from a side (if a side isn't wall) is allowed: movement through a door isn't limited to front-to-back.
- **Describing state** (2026-11-25): a kind's `describe`, `[{when, text}]`, gives words for its state; pointing at one adds each text whose `when` (a formula of its state, default 1) holds, joined by commas (`fixtureDescription`).
- **Use** (U, the piece menu's Use): the cells next to the selected piece with a fixture it can do something to light up; a click does the one thing it can, or, if it can do more than one (a shut door from behind: Open or Bar), a small menu beside the fixture offers them (`V.useChoice`, `App.makeActionMenu`); Esc or a click elsewhere closes it. A piece that could use something keeps the turn from ending by itself.
- **Doors**: `"shape":"door"` draws a feature as a door (`Render.drawDoor`): a thick bar across its cell along its sides, shut; swung round from one end into its back half, open; a shorter bar behind it, barred. **Wooden door** (oak, 15 HP, `state` open and barred: `moveCost` `open ? 1 : 0`, `coverage` `open ? 0 : 100`): open (from any side, when not barred; a step into it opens it), close, and bar and unbar from the back only; broken, it leaves a **Broken door** (passable, 1.5×). Pointing at it says its state: "Wooden door (oak) · HP 15/15 · DR 5 · shut, barred". A barred door can't be opened, so a step into it does nothing (Attack, F, breaks it, if the piece can). Locks (from front or back, with a key or lockpicks) and double doors (two doors side by side, each the other's side, opening together) are to come. **Doors in pathing** (2026-11-25): a shut door a step would open (`Fixtures.stepOpens`: an `onStep` action whose `when` holds, from any side; so not a barred one) is on the way for Go to, Explore and the enemies, counting an action more (the step that opens it): Go to / Explore's routes (`_stepCosts`, on a door you've seen) and the enemies' field (`distanceField(…, openDoors)`) go through it. Go to and Explore step into it, which opens it (`navStep`), then on through. The squad planner has an **open** action (`{id, dx, dy, open:true}`: a piece next to a shut door nearer the player opens it, for an action, gaining as a step of approach), and the plan's later steps go through it (each plan state keeps the doors it has opened); carried out by `Fixtures.enemyStepAction`, which does only `onStep` actions (enemies don't close, bar or unbar). A marching squad with no step that gets it closer opens a door on its way (before trying a jump). Enemies may use any side a fixture's actions allow (`fixtureActions`), and don't need it revealed.
  - **Placing them**: the proximity generator puts doors (`doorTable`, wooden door) in a solid wall's gap at `doorChance`% (30), each cell of the gap its own door, its sides along the wall, facing either way at random (clumps of solids and holes may still land beside a gap, as before). The caverns put a **doorway** (`doorway`) where a tunnel leaves a cavern at `doorChance`% (15): a little past the cavern's edge, a straight wall of rock across the tunnel along whichever of the grid's lines is most nearly across its path, out to its edges, with the door in the middle on the path, its back toward the cavern; not where the cavern is a junction or a join, nor in a tunnel with a stream, and none if it could be gone round or wouldn't shut the tunnel (any of it in or by anything else open, or by the start, or no open tunnel in front of the door and behind it). From the seed, so a cell comes out the same whatever order it's made in.
- **Using a tile:** only your pieces set tiles off, by **entering** the cell (a step, being carried, a jump, a swap, being placed there); standing on one does nothing more. Harm tiles go first, then heal, then loot. Entering one reveals it and shows its magnitude. **Heal** heals your pieces in its area (each one's surplus flows along its links, up to the nearest damaged ancestor, then down to the most damaged child or the branch with the most missing HP, alternating; what nobody needs stays in the heal: it gives only what's needed, and if nobody needed any it isn't used at all, not even a use of a `once` or `count` heal). **Harm** damages every piece in its area, enemies included. **Loot** gives you gold for each of your pieces in its area; `Game.lootTaken` counts the gold taken and `Game.lootOpened` the loot tiles emptied or used up (goal values `lootTaken` and `lootOpened`).
- Subtypes so far: heals **Heal** (1–4, all at once; the default), **Healing spring** (infinite, 1 a time, gone 10 turns after it's found) and **Salve** (6, 2 a time to each of your pieces next to it, pool shown); harms **Harm** (1; the default), **Hidden trap** (2, found by stepping on it) and **Mine** (found within 2; 2 at the centre and 1 next to it, once); loots **Loot** (10–30; the default) and **Cache** (30–60, found within 2, what it holds shown within 1).
- These replace (2026-11-21) heal tiles worth ⌊unvisited neighbouring quadrants ÷ 2⌋ + 1, the countdown every tile started with (shown in a dashed outline; a tile arming under a piece took effect), harm damage and loot score settings, and the heal combo.
- Enemies never step onto harm tiles you know of, and they path around them. (Harm tiles only hurt what you set them off on, so they needn't know of hidden ones.)
- **Solid tile** (dark grey hex): no piece can move onto it or be placed on it, and nothing spawns there; a group can't move if any member would land on one. Solid tiles are there as soon as their quadrant is generated.
- **Solids are made of materials** (2026-11-20, `model/tile/Solids.js`). `data/environs.json5` lists `"materials"` (`name`, `hardness`, `density`, `description`; density isn't used yet) and solid subtypes, `"solids"` (`name`, `composition`: a material, `hp`, and optionally `fillColor` and `borderColor`, any CSS colour or a `--variable`, drawn in place of the usual solid grey). A subtype's **DR** is its material's hardness; its HP is its own, since that depends on how much material there is. A material may have an **`opacity`** (0–100, default 100) and a subtype a **`coverage`** (0–100, default 100: how much of the cell the material fills, e.g. bars or a net); the subtype's opacity is the two multiplied (2026-11-21; see Fog of war). See-through materials so far: glass (10), crystal (15), plastic (30) and ice (50), plus rope for a net (coverage 30) and iron bars (iron at coverage 20). A see-through solid is drawn see-through: its fill at its opacity (but at least 25%, so it still shows), its edges in full; the status line on hover adds "· N% opaque". Walls are made of the `wallSolid` subtype and clumps of `clumpSolid` *(settings, so maps choose them)*; each solid tile keeps its `subtype` and its `hp` (saved). Before environs.json5 has loaded there are no subtypes, so the App loads it before any game (a saved one too).
- **Hole subtypes** (2026-11-21, `model/tile/Solids.js`, `hc.holes`): `data/environs.json5` lists `"holes"`, each with a `name`, a `description` (no material) and optionally a `fillColor` (any CSS colour or a `--variable`, in place of the usual near-black). Hole walls are made of the `wallHole` subtype and clumps of `clumpHole` *(settings)*; each hole tile keeps its `subtype` (saved). Hovering a known hole shows its name and description.
- **Breaking solids:** your pieces (not enemies) can attack a solid like an enemy, by moving into it, but only if their most damage is more than its DR after their penetration. A hit does the attacker's damage roll less that (since 2026-11-24; it was its rank less the DR); at 0 HP the solid is gone, leaving open floor, and the attacker advances into it. In a group move, each piece that would land on a breakable solid hits it. A solid that's too hard just blocks, as before. A damaged solid shows a bar of its HP left, and pointing at a solid says what it is, its material, HP and DR.
- **What a broken solid leaves** (2026-11-24): its kind's `floor` (`data/environs.json5`'s `solids`, a floor id; inherited through `extends`), else the map's base floor. Stone walls, boulders, sandstone, cave rock, stalagmites, stalactites and rubble leave **loose stone**, hedges **dirt floor**, ice walls, ice boulders and icicles **ice**; the rest (wood, metal, glass and so on) leave the base floor. A solid naming a floor that isn't there gets a warning at load and leaves the base floor.
- **Hole tile** (near-black hex with lines fading in from its corners): blocks movement, placing and spawning exactly like a solid tile, but **doesn't block sight**.

## Caverns
(2026-11-22, `model/map/tile/CavernsTileGenerator.js`, the "caverns" tile generator; the **Caverns** map uses it, with "caverns" spawning since 2026-11-24.) A network of caverns joined by tunnels, in solid rock. There are no quadrants: the visited tint doesn't show, and the `quadsVisited` goal value stays 0.
- **Caverns** sit at sites on a hidden lattice (axial, spaced so no two caverns touch: from `cavernSizeMax`), each moved up to 12% of the spacing at random. **Crowding** (2026-11-22, `crowding`, 0) packs them closer: the spacing is cut by up to 60% (at 100), so they touch and then overlap, and tunnels get shorter. With the default sizes, open cells (caverns' share of them) go from 26% (46%) at 0, through 40% (63%) at 50 and 56% (75%) at 75, to 85% (89%) at 100. The lookups (see `siteReach`: how many sites out from a block's can reach it, 2 or more) grow to suit. A cavern is `cavernSizeMin` to `cavernSizeMax` cells (60 / 200), irregular: its radius wobbles with the angle (three random harmonics). Site (0, 0)'s cavern isn't moved, so you start in its centre.
- **Junctions** (2026-11-22): `junctionChance`% (20) of sites (never the start's) have no cavern, just a point where their tunnels meet, moved up to 30% of the spacing. With two tunnels a junction is one long tunnel; with three or more, a fork; with one, a dead-end tunnel.
- **Tunnels** run from site centre to site centre. Every site has one to a neighbouring site nearer the start (its parent, picked by the seed), so they're all joined; any two neighbours that aren't dead ends get another at `loops`% (30). A tunnel is a path walked a quarter of a cell at a time (2026-11-22; before, it swung side to side off the straight line, always heading onward). It heads for waypoints in turn, then the far end: 1 + 4 × tangle of them (`tangle`, 40, as a fraction), spread evenly along it, each moved aside by up to 30% × tangle of its length and onward or back at random; from about tangle 30 one can be behind the one before, so the tunnel turns back for a while (about 30% of tunnels at tangle 50, nearly 90% at 100), and it may cross itself. As it walks it sets off up to 60° × tangle to one side, turns in two waves (up to 0.5 × √tangle radians per cell), and is pulled toward its waypoint, harder the longer that leg goes on; it goes straight for the waypoint when within 2 cells of it, after 3 times the leg's length, or if it strays more than 45% of the tunnel's length to the side (or 20% beyond either end), which keeps it within the lookups' reach. With tangle 0 it's straight. Its width drifts between `tunnelWidthMin` and `tunnelWidthMax` (2 / 3). Its cells are those within half its width (at least 0.6) of points along its path no more than a quarter of a cell apart, so even a 1-wide one at tangle 100 is never broken (every point is within 1/√3 of a cell's centre; before 2026-11-22 the points were spaced along the straight line, and thin, tangled tunnels had gaps).
- **Collapses** (2026-11-22): at `collapseChance`% (0) a tunnel (not one joining two caverns) is blocked all the way across, 30% to 70% of the way along, for 1.5 to 3 cells, by rubble (`rubbleSolid`: rubble, DR 2, 6 HP, 70% coverage, so you can see over it a little). Pieces of rank 3 and up can break through it, so it never cuts the map off for good.
- **Dead ends** (2026-11-22): a site with only the tunnel to its parent. Each kind has a schema and a factor: `cavernDeadEndSchema` / `cavernDeadEndFactor` for caverns, `tunnelDeadEndSchema` / `tunnelDeadEndFactor` for junctions (random / 33). A site *wants* to be one by its schema: `"random"`, at the factor (%); `"adaptive"`, likelier the more open the ways around it (its neighbours whose own dead-end rolls are in the top half): the factor with 3 open, twice it with 6, never with none. More schemas can be added. A site that wants to is a dead end only if no neighbour has it as parent: sites pick a parent that doesn't want to be one when they can. Every site keeps a parent, so nothing is ever cut off, which also caps them: a factor of 33 makes about 14% of sites dead ends, and no factor makes more than about 20%. How a dead-end tunnel ends is picked from **`deadEndTable`** (2026-11-22; simple 2, lasso 1, fork 1, knot 1), and the tunnel's tangle carries on into its shape: **simple**, it just ends; **lasso**, it carries on into a loop and back, 4 to 10 across, its radius wobbling in and out with tangle, as narrow as `tunnelWidthMin`; **fork**, 1 to 3 branches (4 to 9 long, as narrow as `tunnelWidthMin`) off to either side, from up to 6 back along the tunnel so they don't fan out from the end; **knot**, 2 or 3 short, thin branches (2 to 5 long) from near the end, most ending in a small loop (3 to 5 across). (Before, there was only the lasso, a plain ring, at `tunnelLassoChance`%.)
- **Cavern loops** (2026-11-22): at `cavernLoopChance`% (15), a cavern has a tunnel that leaves it and comes back into it 60° to 120° round (a curve from its centre, out past its edge and back).
- **Joined caverns** (2026-11-22): at `cavernJoinChance`% (15), two caverns with a tunnel between them are joined directly instead, if they can be made to touch: each moves toward the other by half the gap (all of it toward the start's cavern, which doesn't move, or in a network its parent; at most 30% of the spacing), and only if that leaves no more than 2 cells of rock between them (ignoring their wobble) are they joined, by a straight tunnel 1.4 times the smaller one's radius wide (at least `tunnelWidthMax`); otherwise it's an ordinary tunnel. A cavern pulled toward several others may end up too far from one, and then that one isn't joined either. (Before 2026-11-22 they were always joined, and a gap left over became a wide straight channel, much wider than any tunnel.)
- **Streams** (2026-11-25; off by default, `streamChance` 0; the Caverns and Ice caverns maps use 3): `streamChance`% of sites are **springs**, and from each a stream **wanders** along the tunnels from site to site, in **any direction**: at each site it goes on down a tunnel it hasn't come along, picked at random, and at `streamBranchChance`% (25) down another too. Each branch runs through at most `streamLength` sites (8, at least 2), and ends sooner where it gets to a site the stream has reached already (it **rejoins** itself; streams from different springs merge too), at a pool that ends it, at a dead end, or where there's no tunnel left. So every cavern or tunnel with a stream has another with it next to it. It's a channel of `streamFloor` (**stream**, moveCost 1.5) down the middle of each tunnel it takes, `streamWidth`% (70) as wide (no wider than `tunnelWidthMax`, at least `MIN_REACH` either side so it has no gaps), so wider tunnels keep dry banks; tunnels run centre to centre, so it crosses each cavern through its middle. A dead end it runs into is wet to its end, the dead end's **shape** too (lasso, fork, knot). **Pools**: a cavern it runs through has one at `poolChance`% (40), of `poolFloor` (**pool**, moveCost 2), `poolSizeMin` to `poolSizeMax`% (15–40) of the cavern, its cells nearest the middle; at `poolTerminusChance`% (30) it's where the stream ends (not at a spring). Water wins over floor patches, stalagmites, stalactites and holes (none on it, nor clumps from it); the start cell and its neighbours stay dry. A tunnel with a stream **never collapses** (rubble would have to let the water through once dug out: left for later). Measured, `streamChance` 3 wets about 18% of sites and 12% of tunnels; making 600 blocks took 0.55–0.62 s with streams on against 0.41–0.52 s off.
  - Without a `maxSize` it's all from the seed (`spring`: each spring's stream worked out once), so a cell comes out the same whatever order it's made in: a branch gets at most `streamLength` sites from its spring, so for a block only the springs that near are looked at (`streamsAround`).
  - In a **network** (`maxSize`), streams are worked out as sites are added (`addStream`), since what's added later depends on where you go: a site added from one whose stream can still run on carries it (always if it's the first to, so a stream goes on while it can; at `streamBranchChance`% if not), or else is a spring at `streamChance`%. A new tunnel to a site already in the network has the stream along it only if that site has one too: that site may have been made already, so it never gets one later. A stream whose site never gets another added just ends there. Saved with the network (each site's count of sites left, and the tunnels with a stream).
- **Rock** (`rockSolid`: cave rock, limestone, DR 8, 40 HP) fills every other cell, so digging is possible but only with damage over 8 (or the penetration to match).
- On **cavern floor**, off the tunnels' paths through the caverns and the cells around the start (so nothing cuts a cavern off): **stalagmites** (`stalagmiteSolid`; calcite, DR 3, 8 HP), **stalactites** (`stalactiteSolid`; calcite, 6 HP, 40% coverage: they hang from the ceiling, so you can partly see past them) and clumps of 1 to 3 **holes** (`holeTable`: pit 6, chasm 3, sinkhole 1). On any open cell but the start: **heal, harm and loot** tiles (`healTable`, `harmTable`, `lootTable`). The `*Frequency` attrs are tiles (for holes, clumps) per 1000 cells: stalagmites 30, stalactites 20, holes 15, heal 4, harm 4, loot 2.
- **Limited size** (2026-11-22): with `maxSize` (open cells; 0, the default, for no limit, up to 50,000) the caverns are a network grown from the start's instead of the endless lattice above. Each site added is next to one already in it (its parent, joined by a tunnel; loops to its other neighbours in it at `loops`%), until the network's open cells (caverns, tunnels, dead ends' shapes, cavern loops) reach `maxSize`; the last site may go over. Sites never added are rock, so its edge is all dead ends. Which site next: at `sprawl`% (0) one next to the newest site that can still grow, otherwise any next to one that can, so 0 grows a roughly round blob and 100 long branching caves. A site that would be a dead end (by its schema) is one only if some other site can still grow, so growth never stops short. `growth`% (100) of `maxSize` is grown when the game starts, from the seed; the rest as your pieces come near the network's edge (before a block is made, the sites that may reach it are grown into, as far as the size allows, and any left are settled as rock), so that part depends on where you go. Even at 0, what's near the start is grown at once. In a network a cavern is joined directly only to its parent, and moves all the way to it. The network (sites with parents and dead ends, sites settled as rock, tunnels) is saved with the game; growing 6,000 cells takes about 50 ms.
- Cells are made as your pieces go, out to where they could see, a block (radius 4) at a time; without a `maxSize` (or with `growth` 100) each cell comes from the seed alone, so it's the same whatever order it's made in. The generator keeps which blocks it has made, and its network if it has one.
- Rock fills every cell made, so a long game holds many solid tiles (about 10k after a lot of exploring, ~0.5 MB saved). If that's too much, only the rock next to open space could be made, and more when one is broken.

## Caverns spawning
(2026-11-24, `model/map/spawn/CavernsSpawnGenerator.js`, the "caverns" spawn generator; kept simple, to inform making spawn generators composable once there are two.) Enemies wait in the caverns and tunnels, as named enemies (see Named enemies). It needs a tile generator with `regionAt(x, y)` (the cavern or tunnel a cell is in, as the seed makes it: `{kind, id, cells, size, start}`; a cavern counts first where a tunnel runs into one; dead ends' shapes and cavern loops count as tunnels) and `makeRegion` (makes its cells' tiles, so what's open there is known before spawning).
- **When**: after your pieces move, the first time any of them is in a cavern or tunnel (not the one you start in; remembered with the game, so going back can't farm spawns), a roll against that kind's chance: `cavernSpawnChance` (40) or `tunnelSpawnChance` (15) to start with. After each roll the chance changes by `cavernSpawnChanceModifierOnSuccess` (-10) if a band spawned, or `…OnFailure` (+10) if none did (and the tunnel ones, -5 and +5), kept from 0 to 100 and saved with the game. A roll that picks a band that fits nowhere counts as none.
- **What**: `enemyChallenge`, a formula (an **expr** attr: numbers, `+ - * / % ^`, parentheses, and JavaScript's Math functions but `random` (`max(a, b)` or `Math.max(a, b)`; until 2026-11-25 only `min max floor ceil round sqrt log abs`); since 2026-11-25 also comparisons `< <= > >= == !=`, `&& || !` (each 1 or 0; a true/false variable counts as 1/0) and `c ? a : b`, looser than arithmetic, the conditional loosest; checked when the map loads) of `level` (your highest), `levels` (your pieces' levels together), `distance` (steps from the start to the piece that went in) and `size` (the cavern's or tunnel's cells), rounded: default `level + floor(levels / 5) + floor(distance / 300) + floor(size / 100)`. That picks from `challengeTable` (a **tiers** attr: `[{min, max, table}, ...]`, each table weights for enemies and groups; the first tier the challenge is in, the highest if it's above them all, none if it's below them all): by default (2026-11-24, eased for a single piece) only goblins (0 to 2), goblins and pairs (3 to 4), mostly pairs (5 to 6), pairs and trios (7 to 8), trios and packs (9 to 12), packs and hordes (13 to 16), and mostly hordes (17 and up). One pick, one band: a bigger set is a bigger group.
- **Where**: on its cells you can't see, as far from the piece that went in as can be; failing those, its other cells, as far from your pieces as can be; clear of your pieces as for any spawn (see Named enemies).

## Fog of war
- *(On; `VISION.fog` in `hc.cfg` turns it off, for testing. It's not a setting: it's a core feature.)* A cell is **in sight** if it's within the **vision radius** (12 steps, *setting*) of one of your pieces and the straight line between the two cell centres isn't blocked by a solid tile. The two end cells don't count, so you see the solid tiles that block you. A line running exactly along the edge between two cells is blocked only if both are solid.
- **See-through solids** (2026-11-21): a solid's **opacity** (0–100%: its material's `opacity` × its subtype's `coverage`, see Solids) says how much of the sight past it gets through. Along the line, each solid at distance *d* cuts what's left of the sight to its share: range = *d* + (range − *d*) × (1 − opacity), starting from the vision radius; a cell is in sight if it's within the range left when the line reaches it. So an opaque solid (100) blocks as before; with radius 12, iron bars (20%) two cells away let you see to 2 + 10 × 0.8 = 10, and two ice walls (50%) multiply. Along an edge, the clearer of the two cells beside the line counts. See-through solids still block movement and jumps, and can still be broken.
- **Enemies** (and their links and effects) show only on cells in sight. **Tiles** show once they've been in sight, and after that always show their current state.
- Cells out of sight are dimmed.
- Only you are limited: the enemy AI still sees everything.
- Sight is worked out again only when your pieces' positions, the tiles or the vision radius change.
- First version, to playtest radius 6 against 12. Possible next steps: markers where enemies were last seen, remembered tiles shown as last seen, denser solid clumps.

## Enemy pathing and spawning
- A distance field is computed each enemy turn (radius 30 around your pieces, avoiding active harm, solid and hole tiles), over the grid's neighbour directions and jumps over gaps (holes, harm): a step counts 1 and a jump 2 (it takes a turn, and the piece sits out the next), so jumping is never quicker than walking but crosses holes (and harm).
- Spawns: a random cell 6–9 steps from the core (a ring on either grid), the squad leader at least 4 steps from every player piece and the rest at least 2; never on a harm, solid or hole tile.

## Maps
(Called levels until 2026-11-21. In code a single map is `gameMap`, not `map`, so it isn't mistaken for JavaScript's `Map` or `.map()`.)
- **The data files** (2026-11-26): the game's data is `data/init.json5` and the files it **includes**, its `"include"`, a list of paths in `data/`: `rules.json5` (the rules' defaults), `environs.json5` (materials, solids, the light, holes, floors, fixtures, status effects) and `entities.json5` (species, backgrounds, enemies, enemy groups). An included file can include more in turn, so any can be split further (environs into solids, holes, floors... say) without code changes. Their tables (top-level properties) are merged into one (`Maps.loadData`); a table in more than one file is an error (the first kept), as is a file included twice (a loop, say); if any file can't be loaded, none of the data is. `loadIndex` then defines everything from the merged tables, grouped as rules, environs and entities.
- `data/init.json5` lists every map, in order, with what a list of maps needs: `id`, `name`, an optional `number` and a `description`. Its `defaultMap` is played when there's no saved game. Each map's own file, `data/gamemap/<id>.json5`, is loaded only when it's played and holds what's needed to play it.
- A map file (2026-11-22) has:
  - `"tileGenerator":{"type":..., ...attrs}`, `"spawnGenerator":{"type":..., ...attrs}` and `"enemyAI":{"type":..., ...attrs}` (all required): the components that make its tiles and its enemies and play the enemies' turn (see Map components), by the names they're registered under, and values for their attrs. A map naming a type that isn't registered, or without one of them, isn't loaded ("Bad map" in the console; the Maps dialog says it couldn't be loaded).
  - `"rules":{id:value}` (optional): gameplay values in place of their defaults (see Settings).
  - `"goals"` and `"penalties"`, and a `"seed"` (all optional).
  
  Rules and attrs it doesn't give are at their defaults; ones that can't be used are skipped with a warning. The player never sees or changes them. A game remembers its map (its rules and components, with the attrs the file gave) and saves it, so a saved game plays the same even if the map file changes; a saved game also keeps each component's full attrs. (Before 2026-11-22 a map file had flat `attrs` for all of these; before 2026-11-21 a map could mark one `pref` to show it in Settings.)
- **Endless Play** names "proximity" for both generators and "squad" for the enemy AI, and sets nothing else, so it plays as the game always has; it has no goals and no end. Maps don't have to be played in order.
- The **Maps** dialog (map button in the header) lists them all; pressing a map's name opens its brief. The info panel shows the map being played and its seed.
- The **map brief** (`view/MapBrief.js`) comes before every new game: from Maps, New game, Play Again, and on opening HexCreeper with no save (help follows it on a first visit). It shows the map's description, its goals and penalties, and a **seed**, filled in with the map's `"seed"` if its file has one, else a random number under a million. A note warns when starting abandons a game under way. Start begins the game with that seed (empty or not a number: a random one); Cancel puts the game's own map values back and it carries on.
- **Seeds** make map generation repeatable: terrain, border walls, tiles and the starting enemies come from `Game.rngFor(...parts)`, a mulberry32 generator seeded from a hash of the seed and the parts (e.g. `'terrain', quadrantKey`), so a quadrant comes out the same whatever order quadrants are made in. Waves, the AI and everything else during play still use `Math.random`. The seed is saved with the game. Not quite everything repeats: tiles skip cells pieces are standing on when the quadrant is made, and settings changed mid-game change what's made afterwards.
- **Goals and penalties** (`model/Goals.js`): a map file may list `"goals"` (things to achieve) and `"penalties"` (things to avoid). Each has a `name`, a `description` and an `expression`, and optionally `progress` (text with expressions in braces, e.g. `"{kills}/40 destroyed"`; numbers that aren't whole show to one decimal place), `lockOnSuccess` (default false: once the expression is true, it stays true and isn't evaluated again), `lockOnFailure` (the same for false) and `required` (default true). They're read into `map.objectives` (`goals.fromMap`, which refuses the map if one is bad), kept with `game.gameMap` and saved, along with each one's state `{value, locked, progress}` (`Game.objectives`). Progress is worked out with the value, so once an objective locks its progress stays as it was (e.g. "30/30 steps" even if the core goes back).
- They're evaluated (`Game.evaluateObjectives`) when a game starts, after each player action and after each turn. The map is **won** when every required goal is true and every required penalty false; it's **lost** when a required goal is locked false or a required penalty locked true, since it can't be won any more, or when the core is destroyed. Either ends the game (`Game.outcome` 'won' or 'lost', `Game.lostBy` the objective that lost it). Optional ones only show how you're doing. A map with nothing required, like Endless, can't be won. Winning offers Play Again or Maps.
- **Expressions** (`model/Expr.js`) are a small JS-like language, parsed by our own code so an expression from a map or an imported save can reach only what it's given: numbers, `true` / `false`, `+ - * / %`, `< <= > >= == !=` (`===` / `!==` mean the same), `&& || !`, `? :`, parentheses and calls. Values: `turn`, `gold`, `coreHp`, `coreMaxHp`, `coreRank`, `coreX`, `coreY`, `coreDist` (steps from 0,0), `pieces`, `maxRank`, `piecesLost`, `piecesPlaced`, `enemies`, `kills`, `quadsVisited`, `lootOpened`, `lootTaken`. Functions: `dist(x, y)` (the core's steps to a cell), `min`, `max`, `abs`. With the core gone, positions are where it was last. `Game.kills`, `piecesLost` and `piecesPlaced` count pieces destroyed (any way) and placed, `lootOpened` loot tiles emptied and `lootTaken` the gold taken from them; all are saved.
- The brief lists the goals and penalties; the info panel shows each with how it stands: a goal ✓ met, ○ not yet, ✗ failed for good; a penalty ○ avoided, ! incurred, ✗ incurred for good; "(optional)" and "· locked" where they apply. Under it, in small text, come its description and its progress.
- Example maps: *Open Ground* (no walls, few solids, waves every 2 turns; goal: take the core 30 steps out, optionally destroy 40 enemies; penalty: lose more than 15 pieces), *The Warrens* (radius-4 quadrants, 90% walls with 1-cell doors, vision 8; goals: visit 12 chambers and last until turn 30; penalty: 30 enemies on the board at once) and *Hedge Maze* (radius-2 quadrants, 90% hedge walls with 1-cell doors, vision 8; goals: visit 24 chambers and open 3 loot tiles).
- Still to come: more kinds of goals (e.g. places to reach shown on the board), end briefs, going on to the next map, and locking maps.

## Settings
Values that stand in for defaults come in three kinds (`model/Settings.js`, `model/map/MapComponent.js`):
- **Preferences** (2026-11-21) are the player's, for the whole app: shown in Settings (the gear in the header), saved in the browser (`hc.prefs`, `{id:value}`, only those changed from the default), never set by a map, and not in a map's brief. They're under **Display**: **Animate pieces** (on; `ANIM.on`): off, nothing is animated and the enemies' turn shows at once; **Animation speed** (100%; `ANIM.speed`, 2026-11-24; see UI); **Show the combat log** (on; `COMBAT_LOG`); **Shadows** (on; `SHADOW.on`, see UI); and **Follow margin** (30; `FOLLOW_MARGIN`, 2026-11-22): how near the edge your core gets before the board follows it, in % of the way from the edge to the middle (see Camera).
- **Map rules** (2026-11-22) are gameplay values that a map's `rules` can set; anything it doesn't set is at its default. ## UI
- **The canvas's size** (2026-11-23): its backing store is matched to its size on the page times the pixel ratio whenever it changes size, from a `ResizeObserver` on it (App), so after layout. A window resize comes before the layout, so sizing from it got the size from before, one resize behind (as opening or closing the browser's dev tools showed), and the canvas showed stretched. A window resize still resizes it when the pixel ratio alone changes (as moving to another screen).
- Pieces show their level (their rank until 2026-11-24) as a symbol inside the border (`drawRankSymbol` in `PieceTypes.js`): 1 nothing, 2 a vertical line, 3 a triangle (point up), 4 a cross, 5 a solid star, 6 an asterisk (3 lines), 7 a 7-point star, 8 four lines (the compass directions), and 9 and up a star with that many points. Lines run edge to edge. The info panel still gives the number. Player pieces are red-orange. Your core has a thick border and every other piece a thin one. The core is bigger by its border (`CORE_BORDER`), so inside the border it's the same size as other pieces. Selection, heal and link-target rings sit a fixed gap (`RING_GAP`) outside each piece's edge, and the core's HP bar sits lower to clear its ring. Enemies are indigo, with indigo link arrows within squads. HP bars (red, `--hp-bar`) show under pieces with more than 1 max HP, and action-point bars (white, `--ap-bar`) under those. Occupied cells are tinted.
- **Moved this turn:** player pieces that have moved (or were just placed) are drawn **inverted**: a light fill with a coloured ring and number. Pieces that can't move because you're out of moves are faded.
- **Selection:** the selected piece and everything it carries turn **amber**, along with the link arrows between them. The selection stays on a piece after it acts (2026-11-21; it used to jump to the next piece that could still move); Tab cycles through your pieces.
- **Link arrows point from each child to its parent**, so they converge on the core or squad leader. Each is a translucent (50%) trapezoid along the line from the child's cell centre to the parent's, behind the pieces, widening towards the parent. It runs from piece to piece, not centre to centre (2026-11-23): each end is cut along the piece's outer edge, curved to follow it, half a pixel in so no gap shows, since a piece drawn translucent (spent, or a ghost) would show a link through it, border and all; an end out of sight (a stub into the fog) is cut straight. Width grows with the longest chain of pieces below the link, so a lone piece's link starts as a point and widths match where links meet.
- Legal moves are highlighted; attacks in indigo. Hovering a move shows a faint copy of the pieces that would move, at their destinations, and tints landing cells that are harm tiles you know of red, heal tiles green and loot gold (a tint only, no border). Hovering an attack highlights every enemy that would be hit.
- **Click** one of your pieces to select it; **shift-click** a cell held by your own piece to move there instead (it falls back to selecting if the move isn't legal). Shift-click also moves in **Free** mode (2026-11-23, `V.clickMoves`): just the piece; where that can't go, in your mode. While Shift is held, the move highlights and the ghost of a group move show what a click would do.
- **The piece menu** (2026-11-21, `view/PieceMenu.js`): clicking the selected piece again opens a small menu beside it (to its right if there's room, else its left; it follows the piece as the board pans): **Character** (I, its character sheet), **Go to** (G), **Explore** (O), **Swap** (S), **Jump** (J), **Attack** (F), **Use** (U), **Rally** (R), **Place** (P, next to this piece), **Attach** (L), **Detach** (X), **Snake** (N) and **Tree** (T), with their costs (in this order since 2026-11-26). Items that can't be used show disabled. Picking one closes it, as does Esc; while it's open, a click anywhere on the board just closes it. The keys work without the menu, on the selected piece. These actions were in the footer, which was getting cluttered.
- **Swap mode** (S): with a piece selected, the pieces it can swap with get a ring; click one to swap.
- **Jump mode** (J): with a piece selected, the cells it can jump to are highlighted (as moves are); click one to jump.
- **Attach mode** (L): with a piece selected, the pieces it can attach to get a ring; click one to attach it. **Detach** (X) acts at once on the selected piece. The info panel shows a piece with no parent as "Detached piece".
- A dashed outline shows the link range around the selected piece's parent.
- **Golden path:** a trail of small gold dots (`--path`, 50% opacity; kept small so it doesn't clutter the board) through the cells your core has been on, in order (`Game.corePath`, saved with the game): only its last `corePathLength` steps (a rule, 500 by default; 0 turns it off; 2026-11-22, before which it was every step since the start), though it always keeps the cell the core is on, or was on last, for the goals. Each step starts at the centre of its cell and stops a gap short of the next cell's centre, where the next step starts, so steps don't overlap; the last step runs all the way to the core. Each step is drawn separately, so steps you retrace stack up and look darker. It's drawn under the fog, so it dims out of sight like the board.
- **Moves are animated** (2026-11-21, `ANIM` in hc.js): a piece slides from cell to cell (`MOVE_MS`, 250 ms, eased); a jump takes `JUMP_MS` (500 ms) and the piece grows by up to `JUMP_GROW` (35%) and back, as if rising towards you. The game state changes at once and the animation catches up, so you never wait: moving a piece again drops what of its last move was still playing. The model records each move (`Game.motionFx`: `{id, from, to, jump, t0, dur}` in `Game.motions`, not saved) and the view draws pieces and links where those put them. In the enemies' turn each squad is a **track**: its actions play one after another (an action takes as long as its moves, or a bump for an attack), so "step in, strike, pull back" reads clearly, and all squads play at once. Effects made during an action (damage numbers, bursts) start when it does, and a piece destroyed in a later action stays drawn until then. A move into or out of sight is animated (the piece shows while moving); one entirely out of sight isn't, and since 2026-11-24 takes no time either (nor does a bump out of sight, `Game.inSight`), so hidden squads hold nothing up and how long the enemies' turn takes gives nothing away. **Animation speed** (a preference, `ANIM.speed`, 100%, 25 to 200) divides every animation time (`hc.animMs`), and so the pace of Go to and Explore. The end-of-game dialog waits for the animation to finish.
- With fog of war on, cells out of sight are dimmed and enemies there aren't drawn. An enemy in sight linked to one out of sight shows half its link (`LINK_STUB`), fading into the fog (2026-11-21; from the piece's edge, as wide all along as at its parent's end, since 2026-11-24: a lone piece's stub, tapering to a point and fading from under the piece, was too faint to see once links got narrower), so an enemy whose rank says it has two children doesn't look as if it has one; its hidden pieces stay hidden. Once the game is over, won or lost, every cell you've seen counts as in sight and every enemy is drawn, wherever it is, so you can see what came for you; cells never seen stay in fog.
- Tiles: heal = green heart; harm = red blood drop; loot = gold coin, no border; each once revealed, with its magnitude bottom right once shown, what's left in it bottom left with `showPool` (∞ if infinite), and a countdown's turns left top right; hovering one with an area outlines the area, and the status line gives its name and description; solid = grey filled hex (in its subtype's colours; solids of the same subtype run together, with no outline or grid line between them, but an edge shows where different subtypes meet); hole = near-black filled hex (or its subtype's `fillColor`); holes of the same subtype run together into one bigger hole, with no grid line between them. Thin lines (as thick as the grid's) run in from the corners on the rim, fading into the fill: from a corner that sticks out, to the hex's centre; from one where the rim turns in (more than 180°), along the edge between the two holes there, following the grid, but only halfway where the rim turns in at both ends of that edge (a choke point), so the two lines don't overlap; none from corners inside the hole.
- **Camera:** when your core comes within the **Follow margin** of an edge of the board (a preference: `FOLLOW_MARGIN`, 30% of the way from each edge to the middle, 5 to 45; until 2026-11-22 a fixed 100 px), it pans by whole hexes until the core is clear of it; a core you've dragged off screen is left alone. Recentering (button or S) and these pans are animated over `PAN_MS` (250 ms); dragging or zooming stops a pan where it is. Starting a game or loading one centres at once.
- **Off-board arrows** (`Render.js`): when your core is off the board, a gold arrow at the edge points at it (from the middle). Since 2026-11-24, each enemy squad you can see (any piece of it in sight) none of which is on the board gets a smaller red one (`ENEMY_ARROW_SIZE`, 75%; `--enemy-arrow`), at its leader, or a piece of it you can see if you can't see the leader. The core's is drawn over them. Since 2026-11-26 the board fills the window, with the header and footer (translucent) over its top and bottom, so "the board" for the arrows is what's between them: `BOARD_INSET_TOP` / `_RIGHT` / `_BOTTOM` / `_LEFT` (the header's height, 0, the footer's height, 0, in pixels); a piece outside that (behind the header or footer too) is off the board, and the arrows run along its edges `ARROW_INSET` (22 px) in, pointing from its middle.
- **Shadows** (2026-11-24, `Render.js`): solids and holes cast shadows from one light, `data/environs.json5`'s `shadow` (`cfg.SHADOW`): `angle` (degrees clockwise from pointing right: 90, straight down), `color` (`#0003`), `blur` (2 px) and `length` (0.5 hexes), which a solid or hole subtype's `shadowLength` overrides (how tall it is, or how deep). A solid's shadow is its hex moved by its length, on the known floor, as dark as its coverage; a hole's is the band its rim casts into it from each edge that faces the light and isn't against another hole. For speed, each is one shape for all the tiles with the same shadow (so overlaps don't darken), and the blur is faked: three fills, a little smaller, as is and a little bigger (for holes, shorter and longer), each a third as dark; the canvas's blur (`filter`, `shadowBlur`) costs a lot every frame, by area, and `filter` isn't in older Safari. **Shadows** is a preference (on).
- **Combat UI** (2026-11-24, App):
  - **Odds on hover:** with one of your pieces selected, pointing at an enemy you can see adds to what the status line says of it (its name and description) your piece's hit chance against it and damage range after its armor (`damageAgainst`), then its against your piece (`status-odds`). An unnamed enemy counts as a creeper.
  - **The combat log** (`Game.combatLog`, see Stats and combat): every attack (hit with its damage, a hit armor soaks, or a miss, each with the roll and the hit chance), harm-tile damage, pieces destroyed (and how many linked below went too), XP and level-ups, as data (who, not the pieces themselves), the last 200 kept, cleared on load and not saved. Its last 8 lines show at the bottom left over the board, newest last, each after its turn; clicks go through it. **K** or the scroll button in the footer shows or hides it: the `combatLog` preference (Settings), so it's kept.
  - **The character sheet** (I, or Character in the piece menu; `view/CharacterSheet.js` since 2026-11-26): a dialog for the selected piece, titled with its name (or "Your core"), species and background. Across the top: its **name** (always editable, 2026-11-28, saved as it's typed and trimmed: a placed piece can be named, and is called by it in the info panel, log and dialogs; emptied, it has none again, but your core keeps its own), its **description** (always editable, saved as it's typed; Enter there is a new line), **level**, XP of what it needs and status. Below, two columns (2026-11-27), each under a heading with a line beneath: **Stats** (the eight, with the points to spend when levelling or creating) on the left, and **Combat** on the right: HP, action points (now/pool), recovery (a turn), initiative, melee accuracy, defence, damage, penetration and armor, all from the rules. With stat points to spend it's for **levelling**: an **Available Points** line at the top of the stats, + and − after each stat (+ puts a point in, − takes one put in back: never below what the stat was when the sheet opened), every value that would change shown with the new one beside it in green (`changed`), and **Confirm** (only once every point is in; spends them) and **Cancel** (puts them back). Without points it only shows, with Close. App gives it `describe(q)` (the lines, for a piece as it is or would be), `preview` (`withStats`) and `onConfirm` (`spendStatPoints`).
- A header bar holds the turn, your gold (`Game.gold`; the game-over message gives all you looted, `lootTaken`), plus Save, Export, Import, New game, Settings, Help and GitHub. A footer holds End Turn, the movement mode and Center; the selected piece's actions are in its menu. Icon buttons (`IconBtn`) show [Lucide](https://lucide.dev) icons copied into `img/icons/` (add one by copying its SVG from the `lucide-static` package), drawn in the button's text colour as a CSS mask; each has a label for screen readers and tests. An info panel at the top left of the board (labels right-aligned, values and bars left-aligned after them, so they line up) shows the map and its seed on one line, then the **selected** piece (2026-11-28; it showed the core's HP before): its name and level (your core by its name; another piece "Linked piece" or "Detached piece"), and a bar each (`HudBar` in App.js: label, bar, value) for its AP (white, `--ap-bar`, a dash at each whole point, as by the piece), HP (red, `--hp-bar`), thirst (blue, `--thirst-bar`), hunger (brown, `--hunger-bar`), fatigue (orange, `--fatigue-bar`; each need with marks at its lower and upper) and XP to the next level (green, `--xp-bar`).
- Settings live in a dialog: the preferences, grouped (numbers as fields, on/off as checkboxes, choices as drop-downs: Animate pieces, Animation speed, the follow margin, Show the combat log and Shadows); values outside the allowed range are clamped; only changed values are saved.
- Help (?) is `data/help.txt`, shown in a dialog; it opens by itself on a first visit.
- **Saving**: asks first, then saves the whole game to the browser, replacing any earlier save. Its tooltip says when you last saved. Opening the game carries on from the save if there is one; a save that can't be loaded (when opening the game or with Load Last Save) is cleared with a message, and then the Maps dialog opens to choose what to play. Starting a new game doesn't delete the save, so reloading the page goes back to it. When your core is destroyed and there's a save, the dialog offers **Load Last Save** instead of Close. You can't save a game that's over.
- **Export / Import** (as in TimeCorps): Export downloads the game as it is now as `hexcreeper-save.json`, the same record as the save, written `compact` (one line, as small as possible; the default) or `pretty` (indented) by `hc.cfg.EXPORT_FORMAT`. The record says which in its `format`; either imports, and so does a record with no `format` (older ones). Saves in the browser are always compact. Import asks first, then reads a save file; if it can be used, it replaces the game and is saved, otherwise a dialog says why and nothing changes.

## Balance notes
- See [PLAYTESTS.md](PLAYTESTS.md) for play sessions.
- **The combat simulator** (2026-11-24, `js/hc/dev/Simulate.js`, `npm run sim`; see the README): a hero (played by a simple bot) against groups from a map's challenge tiers, or named, in an open arena with the map's rules and enemy AI. First run (caverns, level 1, 100 fights a tier): tier 0 (goblins) 98% won with 77% HP left; tier 3 88%; tier 5 86%; tier 7 (trios coming in) 60%; tier 9 33%; tier 13 11%; tier 17 1%. Hit rates were about 45% both ways.
- (Earlier, with both grids.) With the planning AI, a simulated bot that places pieces whenever it can and attacks with every piece that can, but never moves otherwise, lasted a median 11 turns on squares (1 of 10 runs reached the 400-turn cap) and 26 on hexes (none reached it).
- With the old AI, where squads moved as one block, the same bot had 1–2 of 10 runs on squares and 3 of 8 on hexes holding out forever (the **turtling** problem: a stationary rank-4 swarm fed on squads that arrived piecemeal). Patience in the planning AI largely fixes this.
- AI tuning sweep against a wall of pieces over 12 turns: caution 6 → squads brawl (ending exposed 11 of 12 turns); caution 10–15 → a safe strike on 3 turns and exposed on only 3; caution ≥ 9 without patience → squads never commit.
- (Before subtypes.) Heal value rewarded exploring, so the explored interior became less rewarding; harm tiles you'd walked into were gone, but the ones you'd avoided stayed.
- Advance after a kill favours aggressive play; turn it off to compare.

Open questions: what carries over between maps, and whether maps are procedural with per-map rules or partly hand-designed.

## Implementation
- `js/hc/hc.js`: the `hc` namespace and `hc.cfg`: layout and drawing (`PIECE_R`, `OWNERS`...), animation (`ANIM`), and the few gameplay values that aren't rules or attrs (`WORLD_LIMIT`, `SPAWN.clearance`, `VISION.fog`), plus small utilities.
- `model/Grid.js`: `hc.hex`, the hex geometry, the same for every map: `dirs`, `dist`, `center`, `cellAtWorld`, `disk`, `line` (and `lineCellInto`, one cell of it without making anything), `corners`, `edges`, `ring`, `visibleCells`, `keyDirs`, and `keysNear(x, y)`, numeric cell keys (small whole numbers near the given cell) for searches that look at many cells: the enemies' `distanceField`, Go to and Explore's routes and line of sight use them, and look up each cell only once, since making "x,y" strings for every look was most of what they allocated (2026-11-22: Explore had been making some 6 MB of garbage a step). The squad AI also works out no distances when there are no enemies. `makeChunks(k)` returns chunks of radius k (`radius`, `of`, `key`, `neighbours`, `cells`): ProximityTileGenerator makes its quadrants with it (`tileGenerator.quads`), and the game itself knows nothing of quadrants (2026-11-22; before, `Game.reset` set a global `hc.GRID` for the map's quadrant radius). Also `hexDist()` (= `hex.dist`) and `euclid2()`.
- `model/PieceTypes.js`: per-owner `stats`, `steps(piece)` and `draw()`.
- `model/Game.js`: `hc.Game`, a JS.Class (the getters are methods: `getPlayerCore`, `getSelected`); state and rules: `legalMoves`, `act` (advance), `canStep`, `damage`, `moveGroup` → `landOnTiles`, `rngFor`, `piecesMoved` (→ the tile generator, then `revealTiles`), `makeTile`, `distanceField`, `recomputeRanks`, `maxRank` / `moveLimit` / `placeCost`, `applyHeal` (heal tiles).
- `model/tile/Fixtures.js`: `hc.fixtures` (the kinds), `hc.FIXTURE_TYPES` and `hc.Fixtures`, a JS.Module included in Game: `makeFixture`, `fixtureAt` (one you know of), `isHarm`, `revealFixtures` (after your pieces move, from `piecesMoved`), `landOnFixtures` → `useFixture` → `fixtureEffect`, `tickFixtures` (countdowns), `fixtureType`, `turnsLeft`; features: `fixtureValue` (a kind's moveCost or coverage for a fixture now), `fixtureBlocks`, `fixtureMoveCost`, `fixtureOpacity`, `canBreakFixture`, `damageFixture`, `removeFixture` (its floor and destroyInto); facing and actions: `fixtureSide`, `fixtureActions`, `actedState`, `useTargets` / `canUse` / `playerUse`, `stepAction` (used by `Game.playerAct`).
- `model/Effects.js`: `hc.effects` (the kinds: `icon`, `muted`, `hidden`), `hc.EFFECT_EXHAUSTED`, `hc.EFFECT_RALLYING` and `hc.Effects`, a JS.Module included in Game: `effectOn`, `addEffect`, `tickEffects` (housekeeping, after `recoverAp` and `rallyHeal`).
- `model/tile/Subtypes.js`: `hc.Subtypes`, the base class for every set of subtypes from the data (`define`, `get`, `list`; every entry has a name and description, and a parse function gives the rest). Materials, solids, holes, heals, harms and loots are all one.
- `model/tile/Solids.js`: `hc.solids` (`define(materials, solids)`, `get`, `list`, `getMaterial`) and `hc.holes`, Subtypes for the materials, solids and holes from environs.json5.
- `model/Expr.js`: `hc.expr.compile`, the expression language. `model/Goals.js`: `hc.goals` (`fromMap`, `compile`, `isGood`, the values and functions) and `hc.Goals`, a JS.Module included in Game: `getMapObjectives`, `evaluateObjectives` / `endGame`.
- `model/map/tile/CavernsTileGenerator.js`: `hc.CavernsTileGenerator`, the "caverns" tile generator.
- `model/map/MapComponent.js`: `hc.MapComponent`, the base of a map's components (registry, `make`, `cleanAttrs`, `ATTRS`, `this.attrs`). `model/map/Generators.js`: `hc.TileGenerator`, `hc.SpawnGenerator`. `model/map/enemy_ai/EnemyAI.js`: `hc.EnemyAI`.
- `model/map/enemy_ai/SquadAI.js`: `hc.SquadAI`, the "squad" enemy AI: `takeTurn` → `planSquad` (beam search, near squads) / `marchSquad` (far squads). Game's `enemyPhase` calls `takeTurn`. Squad state: `root.idle`.
- `view/View.js`: `hc.view`, the shared view state (canvas, camera and its animated pans `panTo` / `stepPan`, hover, input mode) and cell-drawing helpers (`cellCenter`, `inView`, `addCellPath`, `fillCell`, `strokeCell`, `strokeRegion`).
- `model/Settings.js`: `hc.settings`, the preferences (`get`, `set`, `getGroups`; saved in localStorage) and the map rules (`RULES`; `cleanRules` for a map's, `resolveRules` for all of them, as `Game.reset` and `importFromObj` make `game.rules`). A preference's definition maps to a value in `hc.cfg`. `model/Attrs.js`: `hc.attrs`, declaring and checking attrs (`num`, `bool`, `choice`, `normalize`, `resolve`), used by both and by the generators.
- `model/map/Maps.js`: `hc.maps` (`loadIndex`, `loadMap`), loading the data (`init.json5` and the files it includes: `loadData`) and map files, checking a map's rules and components.
- `model/Persistence.js`: `hc.PersistenceManager`, the save game (from TimeCorps). The record `{version, format, savedAt, data}` (version 4 since 2026-11-26, for stat points; old saves aren't loaded, and no code is kept to load them; the code that loaded older saves was taken out at version 2; a save of any other version is refused, with a message) is stored in localStorage (`hc.save`); `data` is `Game.exportToObj()`, the whole game, and `Game.importFromObj()` restores it. `export` / `importRecord` write and read the same record as a file.
- `view/Render.js` draws the board; `view/GameView.js` is the myt view around the canvas (info panel, status line, mouse input); `view/SettingsDialog.js` builds the preference rows from the definitions (`SettingsList`) and the dialog around them; `view/MapsDialog.js` and `view/MapBrief.js` list maps and brief one; `App.js` is the myt root view (header, keyboard, new games, help; `hc.app.game` exposes the game for tests).
- UI framework: [myt](https://github.com/maynarddemmon/myt), with components, dialogs, i18n and help loading taken from TimeCorps.
