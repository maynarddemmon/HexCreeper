You are a single core on an endless grid. Grow it into a swarm of linked pieces, hold off the enemy squads, and explore for healing and gold. The game ends when your core is destroyed.

<span style="font-size:16px;">YOUR TURN</span>
Each piece acts (moves, attacks, swaps, jumps, attaches or detaches) by spending action points (AP): 1 point an action. Your pieces act in any order, each as often as its points allow. At the end of each turn every piece gets back some points (½ + agl/40), up to its pool (1 + con/10, at least 1), unless it's exhausted (as a jump leaves it); a new piece starts with none. When an enemy first comes into sight it gets (wis + agl)/40 points, and so do your pieces, once a turn. The thin bar under a piece's HP bar shows its points, and a piece without the points to act is drawn hollow. Click a piece to select it, then click a highlighted cell to move it, or use the keys Q E / A D / Z C; W and X go up and down, zigzagging between Q and E or Z and C. Tab cycles your pieces. End Turn (Space) lets the enemies act; the turn ends by itself once nothing can act.

<span style="font-size:16px;">THE SWARM</span>
- Every piece you place links to a neighbour, its parent. Arrows point from each piece to its parent.
- Moving a piece carries everything linked below it, as the movement mode says (M, or Move in the footer). Fixed: all of it moves, keeping its shape. Follow: the piece leads and the whole swarm it's linked to follows in its footsteps, filing through tight places and round obstacles (harm tiles included); only the leader attacks.
- A linked piece must stay within 3 steps of its parent.
- If a piece is destroyed, everything linked below it goes too.
- Swap (S) swaps the selected piece with one of your pieces next to it; their links stay with them. Each spends a point.
- Jump (J) moves the selected piece two cells, over holes and pieces but not solids, onto an empty cell. Nothing linked to it moves, so its links must stay in range. It costs 1 point, and exhausts the piece: it gets no points back at the end of the turn (each jump adds a turn of that). You can't jump in Follow mode. With a piece selected, hovering over a cell it can jump to shows it there as a ghost; click to jump. Enemies jump too.
- Detach (X) breaks the selected piece's link to its parent, however far apart they are; it keeps what's linked below it and moves on its own. Attach (L) links a piece with no parent to one of your pieces next to it. Either costs that piece a point, and changes ranks.
- Shift-click to move just the selected piece, dragging along any linked piece that would end up out of range; where that can't go, it moves in your mode. Shift-click a cell held by one of your own pieces to move onto it instead of selecting it. Holding Shift shows the moves it would make. Alt-click (Option-click on a Mac) a piece next to the selected one to swap them: holding Alt shows the pieces it can swap with, and the pair swapped over the one you point at.

<span style="font-size:16px;">LEVELS AND RANK</span>
Each piece has a level, stats and XP. Killing enemies gives the piece that does it XP, and enough XP raises its level, and its max HP with it. Each level also gives 2 stat points: a piece with points to spend has a gold arrow over it, and its Character menu item pulses. Spend them in its character sheet (I) with + and −, then Confirm. Place costs 4, plus 1 for every 2 levels your pieces have between them.
A piece shows its level as a symbol: level 1 is a plain circle, 2 a line, 3 a triangle, 4 a cross, 5 a star, 6 three lines, 7 a seven-point star, 8 four lines, and from 9 a star with that many points.
A piece's rank is 1, or 1 + the lower of its two highest-ranked children, so a piece needs two children to rank up. A piece that earns XP gets more for its parent's rank, and passes some up to its parent (more for its own rank), which passes some of that on up.

<span style="font-size:16px;">ATTACKING</span>
Moving onto an enemy attacks it: a roll decides whether it hits (from the attacker's str and level against the target's agl and level), then how much damage it does, less the target's armor. When a group moves, every piece in it that would land on an enemy attacks it. Killing an enemy gives XP (and pieces destroyed below a killed piece count too). After a kill (or breaking what it attacked), the attacker advances into the empty cell if it can; to attack without moving, use Attack (F).
With one of your pieces selected, point at an enemy to see the odds: your piece's chance to hit it and the damage it would do (after its armor), then the same for the enemy against your piece. The combat log, at the bottom left, tells what happened in the last few attacks, with each roll and what it needed; K (or the scroll in the footer) shows or hides it. Character (I) in the piece menu opens the selected piece's character sheet: its level, XP, HP, action points, stats, and what they make it in combat.

<span style="font-size:16px;">ENEMIES</span>
Enemies arrive in waves of linked squads, and waves grow with your level. A squad's pieces act with their own action points, as yours do, and it plans its turn: it will carry a piece into reach, strike, and pull back out of reach. Kill a squad's leader to destroy the whole squad.

<span style="font-size:16px;">TILES</span>
- Some floor is harder going: loose stone, mud or ice cost more action points to step onto (pointing at one says how much), though never more than a piece's whole pool.
- A green heart heals, a red blood drop does damage, and a gold coin is loot: gold, the only way to get it (Place costs gold). They act when one of your pieces moves onto them (or is placed on one), and the number in the corner, if it shows, is what each gives. Spare healing flows along links (up first, then down), and any left over stays in the heal. Enemies walk around harm.
- There are kinds of each: point at one to see what it is. Some only show when you're close, or when you step on them; some affect every piece around them (pointing at one outlines where); some last only a few turns once found (the turns left show top right), and some hold more than one go (what's left shows bottom left).
- The trail of gold dots is the path your core has taken; it's darker where you've gone that way more than once.
- Grey hexes are solid: nothing can move onto them or see past them. Point at one to see what it's made of. A piece whose damage can be more than a solid's DR can attack it, doing its damage less the DR, and break through.
- Black hexes are holes: nothing can move onto them, but you can see across them.
- Dashed cells with a number are tiles counting down to appear. Quadrants you've visited are tinted blue.

<span style="font-size:16px;">FOG OF WAR</span>
You only see enemies within sight of your pieces: up to 12 steps away, with solid tiles blocking the view. Cells out of sight are dimmed. Tiles stay on the map once you've seen them. Fog of war can be turned off in Settings.

<span style="font-size:16px;">SPENDING SCORE</span>
- Place (P): pick an empty cell next to the selected piece; the new piece is linked to it.
- Click the selected piece again, or press Enter, for its menu: Character, Place, Swap, Jump, Attach, Attack, Use, Detach and Rally. The arrow keys and Tab move between them. Each has a key too, which works on the selected piece. Go to (G) and Explore (O) send it off by itself, stepping while it has the points, with its swarm following (untangling the swarm, with free steps, swaps and jumps, where it can't follow), and ending the turn when it hasn't, until it gets there, an enemy comes into view, one of your pieces is hurt, or you press a key or click. Snake (N) and Tree (T) get its swarm into formation, detaching, walking and attaching pieces with their points each turn: a snake is a single chain with the piece at its head, which gets through the narrowest tunnels but has the lowest rank; a tree is as near a complete binary tree as it can be, with the highest rank for its size, but a big one can't get through a tunnel a step wide.
- Use (U): the selected piece does something to a fixture next to it, for an action: open or shut a door, or bar or unbar it from behind. With more than one thing it could do, a small menu offers them. Stepping into a shut door opens it (unless it's barred).
- Attack (F): the selected piece attacks a cell next to it, for an action, without stepping: an enemy, a wall it can break, or a fixture with HP, like a barricade or what's left of one, even one you could walk onto. Only it attacks, and it stays where it is.
- Rally (R): a hurt piece rallies, for half an action point: it gets back con/10 × its level in HP at the end of this turn and the next two. It can't rally again for 150 turns less its level (at least 10): its character sheet shows how long.

Save your game with the save button at the top right; it carries on from there the next time you open HexCreeper, and you can go back to it if your core is destroyed. The buttons next to it export your game to a file and import one, and the map button lists the maps you can play. Maps other than Endless Play have goals to achieve and penalties to avoid, listed in the brief and at the top left as you play; meet every goal that isn't optional, without incurring a penalty, to win. Each game starts with the map's brief, where you can choose the seed: the same seed lays it out the same way. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, S to recenter, Esc to cancel. Settings has your preferences, such as whether pieces' moves are animated and how near the edge your core can get before the board follows it.
